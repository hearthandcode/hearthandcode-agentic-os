# Worked Example — Voidbreak Games: Pre-Launch Social Strategy for *Emberwake*

> This is the extended version of the worked example in section 06 of `social-media-strategy`: the complete filled strategy document for the assigned scenario — a solo developer preparing a pre-launch presence on exactly two platforms, Twitter/X and itch.io. It doubles as a calibration artifact: hold your own filled template against this document's shapes and checks.
> Scenario facts: Voidbreak Games is the working alias of a solo developer; *Emberwake* is a hand-painted 2D roguelike about rekindling a drowned lighthouse; demo planned 8 weeks from strategy date; 6 hours per week available; no existing public presence beyond a dormant personal account. All characters, games, and events are fictional.

---

## 0. Inputs (verbatim from the user)

- **Product and pitch:** "Emberwake is a hand-painted roguelike about relighting a drowned lighthouse, one run at a time."
- **Launch state and date:** pre-launch; demo and announcement 8 weeks out (week 8 of the arc); full release targeted next year.
- **Weekly hour budget:** 6 hours (evenings and one weekend block; realistic bad-week budget 4).
- **Ranked audience segments:** players first, dev peers second, small press third (refined in section 2).
- **Constraints:** no Discord before the demo ships (moderation fear, honest); no voice or video of the operator (preference, not a rule); trailer music is licensed for the trailer only, so trailer clips on other platforms are trimmed to 20 seconds; one embargo: the final boss's identity stays unposted.
- **Risk surface:** physics bugs in water rendering (visually obvious if they go wrong); scope skepticism ("lighthouse game about water" reads small to strangers); a late-2025 roguelike crowd already saturated with announcements.
- **Success definition for the next 90 days:** 300 demo wishlists or demo downloads by the end of week 8, via the itch.io page.
- **Nice-to-have recorded:** two peer accounts admired for process-honesty; a screenshot library of 400+ images from development.

## 1. Platform roles and effort split

| Platform | Role | Cadence | Weekly hours | Why it earns the hours |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Twitter/X | primary stage | 3 posts/week + daily replies (≤20 min/day) | 2.5 h | devlog culture, peers, and press read here; conversation compounds |
| itch.io | storefront and archive | 1 devlog/week + page upkeep | 1.5 h | the page persists, converts, and hosts the demo; durability |
| (listening) | sensor | 2 passes x 20 min | 1.0 h | the feed is read, not just written |
| (buffer) | slack | — | 1.0 h | reply overruns, bad weeks, incident time |
| **Total** | | | **6.0 h** | fits the stated budget exactly; buffer is the slack |

- **Dropped or deferred platforms, with reasons/triggers:**
  - YouTube — defer to post-launch: long-form video at 6+ h/week would be the whole budget; dated trigger: "revisit when the demo has shipped and a trailer exists to cut."
  - TikTok — drop: vertical-video cadence conflicts with the operator's stated discomfort; discomfort shows on camera and the format's cadence is unaffordable.
  - Discord — defer: opens with the demo when playtesters need a home; dated trigger: demo launch week.
  - Bluesky — watchlist: mirror X posts, zero incremental production; dated trigger: "revisit at month 2 of the arc."
- **Decisions log entries:** press ranks last pre-launch (small press acts on kits, not attention — kit lands week 7); buffer is sacred (it is the slack rule from section 05, rule 1).

> Check passed: hours sum to 6.0 against 6.0 with buffer; each kept platform has one role; every drop/defer carries a reason or dated trigger.

## 2. Audience segments

1. **Roguelike-curious players** — who: genre players who browse itch.io roguelikes and follow devlog feeds; wants from you: to see the game feel alive, a moment of play; will do: wishlist or download the demo when they see play, not feature lists; type: audience; rank: 1.
2. **Indie dev peers** — who: solo and small-team developers in the same feeds; wants from you: honest process with numbers; will do: reply, boost, offer feedback and playtests; type: community; rank: 2.
3. **Small press and curators** — who: newsletter writers and itch.io curators who cover demos; wants from you: a story and ready assets; will do: cover when handed a kit, not a cold pitch; type: audience; rank: 3.

- **Named disappointment:** small press pre-launch — they receive the press kit in week 7 and otherwise no weekly attention; the plan does not chase journalist accounts. (Decisions log: why press ranks last.)
- **Success-definition link:** the 90-day number is driven almost entirely by players, assisted by peers — so the ranking reads players, peers, press, and the strategy says so in writing.

> Check passed: each card names where the segment actually gathers; the silent-buyer trap is noted (players will stay quiet while peers talk; rank by what moves the goal).

Killed by the four tests: "Marketing" is a goal, not a territory; "Fun stuff" and "Thoughts" overlap everything; "when I have it" is not a cadence; none of the five could name two tired-week example posts. Draft v2:

| Pillar | Share | Definition | Example posts | Bad-week fallback |

- **Cut drafts:** v1's five, each failing sustainability (no cadence), distinctness (overlap), or provability (no artifacts).

> Check passed: shares sum to 100; every pillar has two examples and a fallback; names survive on a calendar.

## 4. Ratio math

- **Weekly content units:** 4 (3 X posts + 1 itch.io devlog) — set by platform cadences, then confirmed against hours.
- **Exact shares:** 40% x 4 = 1.6; 25% x 4 = 1.0; 20% x 4 = 0.8; 15% x 4 = 0.6. Sum: 4.0.
- **Rounding rule (largest-remainder, written out):** take each pillar's floor; distribute leftover units to the largest remainders in order; ties break toward the pillar driving the success definition (build-in-public). Floors 1/1/0/0; leftovers 2 to art (0.8) and build-in-public (tie at 0.6 → success pillar).
- **Per-week counts:** build-in-public 2, systems 1, art 1, community 0 (rolling window).
- **Rolling window rule:** community's 0.6 accrues; over any four weeks it accumulates ≥ 2 units, tracked in the weekly plan grid rather than force-rounded weekly.
- **Hour cross-check:** 3 X posts x ~25 min + 1 devlog x ~45 min + daily replies ~20 min/day ≈ 4.0 production + conversation hours — inside the 2.5 + 1.5 platform budgets plus buffer.
- **Error caught:** the first ratio draft implied 5 weekly units, pricing at 7.5 h against a 6.0 budget; cadence dropped to 4 units before shares were finalized (the order the workflow prescribes: platforms before pillars).

> Check passed: percentages sum to 100; counts match cadence; hours fit budget with replies included.

## 5. Series and formats

| Series | Platform | Pillar | Slot | Recipe (inputs, steps, time) | Fallback | Retirement trigger |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lantern Log | itch.io | build-in-public | weekly, Fridays | build notes + 3 screenshots; write from the week's commit log; ~45 min | two screenshots + two sentences | pause after 8 editions if demo wishlists move <50 |
| One Mechanic | X | systems deep-dives | biweekly, Wednesdays | pick one mechanic; 6-tweet teardown with clips; ~90 min | single-tweet version of the mechanic | pause after 4 editions if median replies <3 |
| Postcard | X | art and world | weekly, Mondays | one image from the library + caption; ~15 min | any library screenshot, one line | retire when the library runs dry; revisit at launch |

- **Cap check:** one series per platform — under the two-per-platform cap.
- **Naming tip in use:** numbered series ("Lantern Log #7") earn recognition and appointment value.

## 6. Engagement rules and boundaries

1. Questions get answered first, within a day, publicly unless privacy requires — public answers compound into FAQ content.
2. Criticism gets exactly one public reply, then stop or move private; never a thread war — thread wars screenshot badly and eat the day.
3. No pricing or feature promises in DMs or replies; dates are said once, when they are real — repeated soft commitments become quotable failures.
4. Never reply hungry, furious, or past midnight; drafts wait for morning — almost every screenshot-level incident starts as a late-night reply.
5. Block criteria fixed in advance: slurs, doxxing energy, bad-faith repetition after one good answer — fixed criteria make blocking policy, not mood.
6. Boost a peer for every self-promotional post (1:1) — feeds that only promote train their audience to scroll past.
7. DMs are closed except to known peers; support and pricing questions route to the itch.io page — response time is invisible until it disappoints, so promise nothing fast.
8. No-go topics: other developers' launches, platform drama, politics, and the final boss — bait resistance is cheaper than repair.
9. Posting hours: X replies only 8-11 a.m. and 8-11 p.m.; the account is off outside them — feeds manufacture urgency that is not real.
10. Log-off signals: scrolling while angry, or drafting reply number four — the session ends when either appears.
11. The known-issues list is public and updated weekly — visible bug tracking converts complaints into trust.
12. Tired-week default: one Postcard + one honest sentence; missing a Lantern Log week requires a one-line written reason in the decisions log — silence is a decision and gets logged.

> Check passed: twelve rules, each with a reason; reply time lives inside platform budgets; the rule the operator flagged as hardest (posting hours) was made stricter, not looser.

## 7. Growth mechanics

- Mechanic 1 — **peer playtest swap** (from midpoint): offer playtest hours and a written bug report; success check: two swaps done and reciprocated by week 8. Hours: within buffer.
- Mechanic 2 — **guest devlog exchange**: offer a guest section in a peer's devlog and host theirs; success check: one exchange live by week 7. Hours: within buffer.
- Mechanic 3 — **watchlist mirroring** to Bluesky: zero-cost presence; success check: revisit trigger fires at arc month 2.
- Collaboration offers (what we give first): playtest hours with written notes; an original tile-set asset for a peer's game; a guest slot in Lantern Log.
- Cross-platform loop: X posts point to the Lantern Log post of the week; Lantern Log points back to X for conversation; itch.io page is the conversion surface (wishlist/demo).
- **Banned shortcuts** (from `references/growth-and-reach.md`, with reasons): purchased followers (poison signal, risk standing); follow-back chains (audiences that want numbers, not the product); engagement pods (comments indistinguishable from silence, corrupt the only honest signal); metric overlay tricks (borrowed attention arrives with a grudge).
- **Honest shape stated:** flat for weeks 1-3, first real lift expected from the week 2 explainer thread or the week 5 demo slice; no lift is scheduled to arrive on time.

## 9. Listening setup

- **Keywords:** `Emberwake`, `voidbreak`, `lighthouse roguelike`, `itch.io roguelike`, two peer titles with overlapping audiences; exclusions: the common noun "wake," other products' names.
- **Cadence:** Tuesday and Friday, 20 minutes, on the calendar; timer runs; unfinished items roll.
- **Buckets and destinations:** praise → testimonials note (quoted with permission later); questions → growing FAQ (recurrences become pillar posts); bugs → known-issues list → backlog owner (the operator); feature asks → monthly-reviewed backlog note; drama → escalation rule.
- **Escalation rule:** a pass becomes a crisis call only when the source is unknown to the operator, the item is spreading beyond its original thread, and the claim concerns safety, deception, or conduct — otherwise normal buckets.
- **First pass finding:** a peer asked publicly how the water rendering worked; it became week 2's One Mechanic thread — the listening→content route working on its first outing.

## 10. Crisis one-pager

- **Tier 1** — one-off criticism from a small account → normal rules: one honest reply, stop; no schedule change.
- **Tier 2** — a pattern or spreading screenshot; influencer pile-on → stop scheduled posts; assess within 2 hours; holding line if needed; second opinion engaged.
- **Tier 3** — safety, legal, or existential (doxxing, threats, conduct claims) → quiet on topic, gather facts, one response with counsel; silence is a decision and gets logged.
- **First-hour checklist:** stop the schedule (both platforms); classify the tier in writing; call the second opinion; preserve screenshots and links; draft the holding line; nothing else on-topic.
- **Holding line skeleton:** "We see this and are looking into it — next update by [specific time]."
- **Do-not list:** argue in threads; delete criticism; subtweet; over-promise; blame users.
- **Apology anatomy (for any statement that owns fault):** what happened; who was affected and how; own the cause without passive voice; what changes with a date; what will not happen again, as a mechanism. No defenses attached.
- **Second opinion (named):** a trusted peer dev who agrees to the role; **Platform for serious statements:** X, where the audience actually is.
- **Rehearsal:** scheduled-post pausing was tested once on both platforms in peacetime; the pager lives pinned in the operator's notes app, not only in this document.
