# growth-and-reach.md — Organic mechanics, collaboration, honesty guardrails

This reference backs workflow step 8. It carries the growth mechanics menu, the collaboration playbook, cross-platform loops, and the banned-shortcuts list with reasons.

## The honest premise

Growth for a small operator is slow and compounding or fast and hollow. The mechanics below are slow: they trade patience for durability. The strategy should say the expected shape out loud — a realistic arc is flat for weeks, then visibly lifted by an artifact (a demo, a thread that traveled, a collaboration) — so nobody mistakes week three's silence for failure.

## The mechanics menu (pick two or three, priced in hours)

**1. Proof-of-work discovery.**
The default engine: consistent artifacts on the primary stage earn shares the operator never asks for. Costs nothing beyond the content plan; the "mechanic" is making artifacts legible — one idea per artifact, a title that says what it is, formats people can reshare without context.

**2. Collaboration exchanges.**
Peer playtest swaps, guest devlogs, shared bundles, art trades. Costs: hours of giving before hours of receiving. Converts: at the rate of trust, which is slow but durable. This is the highest-yield mechanic for peer-dense products and the one most often scheduled too early — approach peers only after the feed shows proof-of-work, and always with an offer, never an ask.

**3. Cross-platform loops.**
Deliberate paths between the operator's own surfaces: the stage feed points to the storefront's long-form post, the storefront page points back to the stage account, the watchlist mirrors. Costs: minutes per post in links and framing. Rule: each surface must give a reason to move (the full recipe, the demo, the kit) or the loop is just noise; and one direction must terminate at the surface that converts.

**4. Series appointment value.**
Named series with fixed slots train anticipation — the audience returns for the next number without the platform's algorithm being asked. Costs: the series recipe's production hours (already in the plan). This is the least appreciated mechanic: it converts consistency itself into reach.

**5. Community rituals.**
Recurring prompts, monthly playtest calls, feedback threads. Costs: hosting time plus the moderation the space earns. Converts slowly, then suddenly: rituals give quiet followers a way in, and members eventually carry the work outward for free.

**6. The visibility window.**
Each platform has moments of elevated attention (launches, festivals, seasonal waves, adjacent big releases). The mechanic is preparation: have the artifact ready so the window can be caught, without pretending windows are plannable. Costs: zero extra hours; the payoff is variance, and the strategy should not depend on catching any.

**7. Collaborator discoverability.**
Press kits, asset packs, and clear product pages make the work easy to cover, embed, and credit. This is growth by removing friction rather than by adding effort, and it compounds with every journalist, curator, or creator who finds the material usable.

Pick mechanics that fit the hour budget; a collaboration pattern that costs a weekend is a special event, not a growth plan. Every chosen mechanic lands in the strategy with its price and its success check.

## The collaboration playbook

1. **Lead with an offer** — playtest hours, an asset, a skill, an audience, a guest slot. Peers collaborate with leverage, not enthusiasm; if the only offer is friendship, the plan is not ready (build proof-of-work first).
2. **Match the stage** — approach peers whose work shows they trade this way; the feed history is the interview.
3. **Make the exchange legible** — one sentence of what, when, and what happens for both sides; vague enthusiasm is the most common reason good offers go unanswered.
4. **Deliver first** — the operator's half ships before or with the ask, never after.
5. **Write it down** — the collaboration goes in the arc with a week, an artifact, and a thank-you post that names the peer.
6. **Schedule past the midpoint** — no collaboration requests before the arc's midpoint; approaching peers before the feed has proof-of-work converts at a fraction of the rate and burns the introduction.

## Cross-platform loop design

- One conversion surface (usually the storefront or list) and one or two feeding surfaces.
- Every loop hop gives a concrete reason: full recipe, build, kit, archive.
- Mirrors are clearly mirrors (same artifact, adapted), so watchlist presence never pretends to be engagement.
- Loops are audited monthly: which hop is actually traveled? Fix the dead hop or delete it.

## Banned shortcuts (copied into the strategy verbatim)

- **Purchased followers** — fake accounts engage nothing, poison the ratio of signal to noise, and can put platform standing at risk.
- **Follow-back chains** — audiences assembled from reciprocity do not want the product; they want the number, and they unfreeze the moment the number stops flowing.
- **Engagement pods** — coordinated comment groups fool the poster, not the audience; the comments are indistinguishable from silence to everyone outside the pod, and they corrupt the only honest growth signal an operator has: what strangers actually do.
- **Metric overlay tricks** — raids, thread-bombing hashtags, reply-guy volume attacks: borrowed attention that arrives with a grudge attached.

Why the bans are absolute rather than situational: each one trades the operator's only real asset — being believed — for a number that does not buy anything. The strategy's success definition is chosen precisely so these shortcuts cannot help it.

## Honesty guardrails for reporting growth

- Report reach with its cost: "the demo thread reached 40k because it was shared by two larger peers" is an honest sentence; "40k reach" as a personal metric is not.
- Distinguish owned reach (list, storefront followers) from rented reach (feed impressions) when telling the user what is actually growing.
- If a mechanic's only defense is "it worked for someone else," it is not justified; growth claims in the strategy cite the operator's own last audit first.

## The growth rubric (run in step 12)

- Two or three mechanics, each priced in hours inside the budget.
- Each mechanic names its success check (what would tell us it is working, seen where).
- The collaboration list has written offers and targets the arc's midpoint or later.
- Cross-platform loops name their conversion surface.
- The banned-shortcuts list appears in the strategy with its reasons.
- Expected growth shape is stated in honest terms — flat-then-lifted, not a curve invented to please.
