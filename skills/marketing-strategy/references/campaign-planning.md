# Campaign Planning

Methodology for turning a strategy into a time-bounded, budgeted, and measurable campaign. A campaign is not a checklist of channels; it is a guided sequence of actions, each directed at a specific audience, with a clear objective.

## Anatomy of a campaign

Every campaign has these five components:

1. **Objective:** A single measurable outcome, not a placeholder. "Sell 200 copies of Focus Forge in the first month" is an objective. "Build brand awareness" is not — it is a step toward something measurable.
2. **Target audience:** Which segment from the STP analysis is this campaign for? One campaign = one primary audience.
3. **Core message:** The Level 1 promise from `references/messaging-frameworks.md`. Every piece of content reinforces a single message.
4. **Flights:** Time-bound phases within the campaign with different approaches (tease, launch, sustain, wrap).
5. **Budget:** Total spend and per-channel allocation. At \$15 per unit, a \$500 spend means you need 34 extra customers just to justify the spend, plus the cost of labor.

## Budget math column

At \$15 per unit sale, every \$1 of marketing cost requires 1/15 = 6.67% of a sale. So a \$500 campaign budget costs 34 sales before the first dollar of profit. This makes "small budget" campaigns especially sharp at indie prices.

| Total budget | Copies needed to break even | Minimum customers per week (single) |
|-------------|---------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| \$300 | 20 copies | 5/week over 4 weeks |
| \$500 | 34 copies | 8—9/week |
| $1,000 | 67 copies | ~17/week |
| $1,500 | 100 copies | 25/week |

For Focus Forge, a realistic launch might set the budget at \$750–80, requiring 50 unit sales to EB before marketing. Total acquisition (email free, social free) means the budget is smaller.

## Template: Campaign Brief

Use `templates/campaign-brief-template.md` to document campaigns.

The sections:

- **Campaign Title:** A specific label (e.g., "Focus Forge / Launch: Productivity Week").
- **Objective with date anchor:** By X date, achieve Y outcome.
- **Target:[check] anchored to one segment from the STP analysis.** Who precisely is the audience?
- **Core message and creative tone/personality guide.
- **Budget and period: What is the invested money and time frame?**  
- **Channel plan:** Which owned, earned, paid channels with the mix.
- **Assets needed:** List of declared deliverables (landing page, social posts, graphics, entry).
- **Success metrics:** Primary KPI and secondary KPI for each channel.
- **Contingency:** If X happens (the platform down, low conversion), we Y.

## Flights

Every product launch that sells software should have at least three flights before the first week:

| Flight | Period | Strategy | Budget share | Goals |
|--------|--------|----------|--------------|-------|
| Seed | Weeks 1–3 pre-launch | Build audience before launch day. Content (posts, sneak peeks) that lead to an email waitlist. No working-only. | 5–10% | List of 100–200 emails |
| Launch | Launch week + 1 week | Full press. Social, PR outreach (if product planned), community posts, all channels active. Measure and stress. | 60–70% | 50–100 unit sales |
| Landing | Week 3–5 post-launch | Convert the remnant burst. Follow-up emails to waitlist, lookalike targeting on social. Optimize conversion. | 20–30% | 25–50 more sales |

## Creative brief

Before produce any copy or visuals, the creative brief documents:

- **Goal (what will this element achieve?)**
- **Audience (audience / type?)**
- **Tone (value, simple, technical? )**
- **Non-negotiables (the one message that can't be cut)**
- **Layout/format** constraints (if known)

Example:

**Creative Brief: ExpertP H1, for Focus Forge**

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Goal | Increase the rate of "I want a single component" recognization from the position. |
| Audience | Freelance writers, Tortured Author audience |
| Tone | Direct, but not — we say what the application blocks without a "distraction distraction" 
| Non-negotiable | The phrase "$15, one-time purchase" appears |
| Format | 300x250 landing text sidebar; no video |

## When to stop

Stop the campaign if, after 3 weeks, cost per acquisition — the lifetime value divided — is > 0.6 (i.e., you spent > 60% of the unit price to acquire one customer). For a $15 product, that means spend-to-acquire <= \$9.

## After campaign checklist

- [ ] Did we hit the objective? (verify with dashboard from `references/marketing-metrics.md`)
- [ ] Which channels produced the lowest CAC?
- [ ] Which messages had the highest engagement?
- [ ] Are any emails or social posts worth reforming into always-on content?
- [ ] Were the contingency turn-triggered? What was learned from failure?
- [ ] Was the budget within the line for the result? Is the management acquiring externally feasible at this rate?.
