# Messaging Frameworks

Pattern catalog for building value propositions, message hierarchies, and proof points. Copywriting itself is not covered — the focus here is structure: what to say and in what order.

## The value proposition canvas

A value proposition is a promise that if the buyer uses this product, they will get a specific benefit. The canvas maps the product's features to the audience's pains and gains.

| Customer segment | Pains | Gains | Product features | Pain relievers | Gain creators |
|-----------------|-------|-------|-----------------|----------------|---------------|
| Freelance writer | Context switching, deadline anxiety, guilt about procrastination | Deep flow, a finished output, pride in work | Single-window mode, no notifications, offline | Blocking social media impossible, task-only interface asymptotic to reducing overwhelm | Session pacing shows accumulated focus as a timeline |

Write this table for each segment. The pain relievers and gain creators are the basis of every headline.

## The message hierarchy template

A message hierarchy is a pyramid:

```
Level 1 (Primary promise)
  └── Level 2 (Supporting message A)
      └── Level 3 (Proof point for A)
  └── Level 2 (Supporting message B)
      └── Level 3 (Proof point for B)
  └── Level 2 (Supporting message C)
      └── Level 3 (Proof point for C)
```

- **Level 1:** The single most important thing the audience should know. Use it as the headline, homepage H1, social media bio.
- **Level 2:** Dimensions of the central promise, each a separate benefit that expands the primary promise. Use as subheads, social posts.
- **Level 3:** Evidence — test, win, testimonial, specific number. Use as body copy, case studies, bulleted feature lists.

### Example hierarchy for Focus Forge

**Level 1 (Primary promise):** "The \ $15 desktop app that kills distractions one window at a time."

**Level 2 (Supporting):** "You open one file, you work on one window, and you cannot open anything else until the session ends."
**Level 3 (Proof):** "When you start a session, all other windows close. Not minimized — closed/hidden behind a wall of the session screen."

**Level 2 (Supporting):** "No subscription fees. You pay once and own the license for both your work and personal computers."
**Level 3 (Proof):** "No upsells, no bundled features, no 'premium' tiers that add the focus tool."

**Level 2 (Supporting):** "It works offline, syncs via Dropbox, and takes 10 seconds to start."
**Level 3 (Proof):** "Download, one-time install; pick a file path; hit start."

## Proof points inventory

A proof point is anything that makes the promise concrete and credible. Categorize yours:

| Type | Example | Strength |
|------|---------|----------|
| Quantitative | Launched 3 years ago, zero subscription price increases | Strong if large numbers; weak if too anecdotal |
| Qualitative | "I have clear block of four-focused hours per day and the product was not the solution" — user testimonial | Strong if named; medium if anonymous |
| Third-party | Mention in a newsletter, review, "Best of Product Hunt" | Very strong — independent validation |
| Feature | “Works with no whitelist — blocks everything before you can make an exception” | High for direct comparison |
| Comparative | "30s setup versus 10-minute whitelist for alternative tool" | Strong if the comparison is fair and measurable |

## The elevator pitch sequence

If you have one sentence, use Level 1 from the positioning statement. If you have one paragraph, structure it as:

1. What problem do I solve? (Pain)
2. How does my product solve it differently? (Promise)
3. Why can you trust it? (Proof)

## Types of messaging by audience stage

| Stage | What they need | Example |
|-------|----------------|---------|
| Unaware | Category pain — they have the problem but associate it with something else | "Dozens of productivity apps promise focus; what if the problem is the apps, not your willpower?" |
| Problem-aware | The problem has a name | "You cannot achieve deep work when your tools allow deep distraction." |
| Solution-aware | The product exists | "Focus Forge blocks everything except the one thing you need to look at — for one session." |
| Product-aware | They know the product but haven't bought | "Try the free trial. The first three focused sessions cost nothing." |

## Message consistency checklist

- [ ] Equals reading: If someone reads three headlines from different channels, do they feel they're from the same brand?
- [ ] Segment fit: Does each message land someone who is not in the target segment but which it inaccurate or worse?
- [ ] Proof available: If anyone says "prove it," do you have an answer?
- [ ] No special language: Avoids terms only the product creator uses.
- [ ] One voice voice: Tone and vocabulary sit along the dimension of the brand — earnest, technical, blunt, whimsical, mechanical — across the hierarchy.

## Evidence of work

A messaging framework is not copy. A headline or social post written from the hierarchy still needs the strategic framework to have existed. Use this table to bridge:

| Level | Strategic output | Tactical application |
|-------|-----------------|---------------------|
| Level 1 | The single claim we own | Homepage H1, paid ad headline, email subject |
| Level 2 | Dimensions of that claim | Blog post title, testimonial blurb, pitch deck body |
| Level 3 | Supporting evidence | Bullet list in copy, case study, "what people say" section |
| Proof spasms: Wherever a competitor says it more loudly, prove something that'd takes balls. | Error catch: If the proof point would cost money or time to generate, it is not proof — it is a promise about future proof.
