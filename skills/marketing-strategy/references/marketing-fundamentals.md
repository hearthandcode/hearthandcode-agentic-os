# Marketing Fundamentals

Methodology reference covering STP (Segmentation, Targeting, Positioning) and the 4Ps reframed for small teams. These are the strategic foundation stones — every campaign, channel choice, and message hierarchy traces back to decisions made here.

## STP: The three-act structure of market strategy

Segmentation, Targeting, and Positioning were formalized in the 1960s but remain the highest-leverage thinking tool in marketing because they force specificity before action. Without STP, a campaign treats every human the same; with it, you know exactly who you are talking to and why they should listen.

### Step 1 — Segmentation

Split the total addressable market into groups that share meaningful differences in need, behavior, or context. A segment is useful when it is:
- **Distinct:** Members within a segment share needs; members across segments have different needs.
- **Measurable:** You can size the segment (number of people, total wallet).
- **Reachable:** You have a realistic channel to speak to them.
- **Actionable:** You can make a different offer or message for this segment.

Ways to segment for a small team:

| Basis | What you look at | Good for |
|-------|------------------|----------|
| **Demographic** | Age, income, job role, company size | B2B, consumer goods |
| **Behavioral** | Usage frequency, feature adoption, purchase history | SaaS, apps |
| **Needs-based** | Jobs to be done, pain points, desired outcomes | Most situations — the hardest and most valuable |
| **Psychographic** | Values, identity, lifestyle | Premium brands, communities |

A solo founder should aim for 2-4 segments. More than five and you cannot afford to serve them all. Fewer than two probably means you are designing for your own reflection.

### Step 2 — Targeting

Choose which segment(s) to serve, and at what depth. Three targeting strategies:

- **Undifferentiated:** Same product, same message, same everything to everyone. Works when the market is homogeneous (tap water). Almost never works for a new indie product.
- **Concentrated (niche):** Serve one segment with a product and message built entirely for them. Lowest cost, highest risk if the segment is too small. The right default for a solo founder: serve one segment brilliantly, expand later.
- **Differentiated:** Serve multiple segments with distinct messages and sometimes distinct products. Higher cost, higher ceiling. Attempt only after niche is validated.

### Step 3 — Positioning

Positioning is how you make the target segment think of your product in a way that is distinct from alternatives. It lives in the customer's mind, not on your website hero header. More detail in `references/positioning-and-differentiation.md`.

## The 4Ps reframed for small teams

The classic framework has four pillars. For a solo founder, big-P Product and Price are mostly designed before marketing begins. Marketing's job is usually promotion with tight constraints on Place.

| P | Classic scope | Small-team scope |
|---|---------------|-------------------|
| **Product** | Features, design, quality, packaging | Know your product inside out; marketing can suggest feature priorities but does not own the roadmap |
| **Price** | List price, discounts, financing | Understand price elasticity for the segments; pricing strategy belongs in close coordination with marketing (e.g. launch discount, annual vs monthly pricing) |
| **Place** | Distribution channels, retail, inventory | Which platforms serve your audience — app store, Gumroad, itch.io, direct web. Each place has different best practices and conversion baselines |
| **Promotion** | Advertising, PR, sales, promotions | Your core activation lever. Everything in the rest of this skill is promotion — but promotion that works only if the other P steps are clear |

### Price elasticity for a $15 indie product

A $15 one-time purchase sits in a sweet spot: below impulse-buy-too-cheap (sub-$5) where quality is doubted, and below considered-purchase ($30+) where the buying cycle takes days. At $15, a good product emerges from of trust and a clear hook. Price experiments: increase to \$20 if value perception is strong (add a "pro upgrade" path), discount to \$10 for limited launch windows to gain initial reviews. A \$5 swing changes conversion meaningfully — test it before setting permanent price.

## Marketing maturity stages

A useful framing for how much marketing a business can absorb:

| Stage | Brand touchpoints | Budget | Goal |
|-------|-------------------|--------|------|
| Unnoticed | 0–100 strangers | \$0–100 | Validate that someone buys |
| Emerging | 100–1k aware | $50–500/mo | Build repeatable acquisition |
| Growing | 500-5k engaged users | $500-5k/mo | Scale what works |
| Established | 5k+ user base | $5k+/mo | Dismantle brand, diversify channels |

A solo founder with a $15 app is almost always in the first or second stage. Act accordingly.

## Key takeaway

Never skip STP thinking. Write down the segment names, their size, and the one-sentence position you want to occupy in their mind before you write a line of copy, run a testimonial, or place a budget. This single discipline saves more wasted spend than any channel optimization ever will.
