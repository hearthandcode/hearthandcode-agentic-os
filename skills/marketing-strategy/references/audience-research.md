# Audience Research

Methodology for understanding who your customers are, what they are trying to get done, and whether a segment is worth pursuing. Without audience research, marketing is shouting in an empty room.

## Interview-driven discovery

For a solo founder with no users yet, interviews are the highest-leverage research activity. A set of 8–12 well-conducted interviews with people in your target demographic will surface more insight than any survey or analytics tool.

### How to recruit interviewees

- Start with your own network — former colleagues, industry friends, online communities you are already part of.
- Use one sentence of context, not a pitch: "I am building a productivity tool for freelancers and want to understand how you currently manage focus during the day. Could I have 20 minutes of your time?"
- Offer nothing in exchange (or \$10 gift card if the network is cold). The interview itself is the value — most people like being asked their opinion about their own domain.
- Aim for 8 total; you will learn minimally less after 5–6 if the segment is narrowly defined.

### The interview protocol

A semi-structured format. Ask open-ended questions; do not lead.

1. **Warm-up:** Tell me about a typical work day. What tools are always open?
2. **Pain exploration:** What parts of your day feel the most fragmented or frustrating? When do you lose focus? What happens next?
3. **Current solution:** When you need to concentrate really deeply for 60 minutes, what do you do? Create — we start, use a tool, change location, do nothing?
4. **Emotional resonance:** How does that lost time feel? Is it a small annoyance or a big source of stress?
5. **Verification:** I am thinking about a tool that would — one sentence product idea. What do you think? Would it help you more than what you currently do?
6. **Segment check:** Do you think others in your role face the same problem? More or less?
7. **Wrap:** What is the best advice someone gave you about staying focused? (Surprising end — reveals values without asking directly.)

Do not market test. Do not ask "would you pay for this?" — people are bad predictors of their own purchasing. Look for evidence that they have already spent time or money fixing the problem and calls to feeling stuck.

## Jobs to Be Done

The Jobs to Be Done (JTBD) framework focuses on what the customer hires a product to do, not who they happen to be.

A JTBD statement: "When [situation], I want to [motivation] so I can [desired outcome]."

For Focus Forge:

- **Segment A — Freelance writer:** "When I have a tight deadline and my usual tools are actively distracting me, I want to lock myself into one document without losing any research I've done, so that I can finish the draft in one sitting without guilt."
- **Segment B — Remote knowledge worker:** "When I need to complete a focused task but my phone and browser keep pulling me away from it, I want a tool that does not depend on my willpower, so that I can deliver quality work."
- **Segment C — Student researcher:** "When I need to write a paper or read dense material, I want to cut off every notification and impulse-based distraction for a defined period, so that the writing becomes the only thing that happens in that window."

### Distinguishing functional from emotional progress

- Functional JTBD: "Get this task done."
- Emotional JTBD: "Feel like I am in control of my attention; reduce shame about procrastination."

The product that addresses emotional progress is higher price and retention than the one that addresses only functional progress. Focus Forge's value prop is emotional: it removes the guilt of a fragmented day.

## Segment sizing

### Top-down sizing

Estimate total addressable market (TAM), serviceable addressable market (SAM), and serviceable obtainable market (SOM) using existing data.

For Focus Forge:
- TAM: Knowledge workers in the US alone — ~80 million. (BLS: business + computing + management, not counting education.)
- SAM: Knowledge workers who identify attention fragmentation as their top productivity blocker — maybe 20% of that = ~16 million.
- SOM (first year): If 0.01% of SAM converts — roughly 1,600 customers at \$15 = \$24,000.

This is a plausible and sobering number for a solo founder launch. Marketing changes these inputs; it does not break the TAM formula. You need one channel that can reach 1,600 potential buyers within budget.

### Bottom-up sizing

Reverse from realistic acquisition capacity:

A single solo founder running a launch can produce:
- 3–5 blog posts
- 2–4 community posts (Reddit, Hacker News, indie forums)
- 1–2 podcast appearances
- A Product Hunt launch (if applicable)
- Via email: 200–500 mailing list signups

Given a 3–8% conversion from email to purchase (typical for a \$15 SaaS product), you arrive at 15–40 eventual customers from launch week, not hundreds. Combine with discovery (search, recommendations, social) where 0.5–1% conversion might deliver 100–300 over the first quarter.

This number is not bad — validation. 100 paying users at \$15 is \$1,500. At \$1,500 you can start learning what they actually need.

## Quick segment viability rubric

| Test | Pass | Barely pass | Fail |
|------|------|-------------|------|
| Segment size (addressable) | 100k+ | 20k–100k | <20k |
| Problem intensity | Active pain, seek solutions | Annoying but manageable | Barely notice |
| Purchase readiness | Already spending on alternatives | Would try a trial | No purchase history |
| Avenues to reach | Dedicated communities, publication, conference | Scattered, hard to find alone | No identifiable cluster |
| Competition | Weak alternatives, known pain | Fragmented or direct competitors | Strong incumbents with lock-in |
| Self-serve viability | Direct sign-up works | Requires sales interaction | Enterprise-only sales needed |

## Winning the interview or survey

- Minimum 3 interviews per defined segment before positioning.
- Record and transcribe (with permission). The actual verbatim quotes are usable in marketing.
- Look for divergence: one interviewee says they think the time is lost; another says they fight for an extra hour. Both are in the same segment.
- After the interview: Rate likely purchase on 1–5 in your own notes. Patterns over 3–4 respondents who score 4–5 indicate traffic.
