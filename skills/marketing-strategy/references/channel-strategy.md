# Channel Strategy

Methodology for choosing and sequencing marketing channels. A channel is any path by which your message reaches your audience. Small teams cannot afford all channels equally — selection is more important than execution.

## The channels accessible to a solo founder

| Channel | Typical cost | Control | Lead time | Best for |
|---------|-------------|---------|-----------|----------|
| Owned (email list, blog, website) | \$0–50/mo hosting | Full | Immediate | Long-term sustainability |
| Earned (PR, reviews, podcasts, community recommendations) | \$0 | None (need to be invited/noticed) | Months | Trust, third-party credibility |
| Organic social (Twitter, Reddit, Hacker News, LinkedIn) | \$0 | Partial | Days to weeks | Reach and community building |
| Paid social (Meta, Reddit, LinkedIn ads) | \$300–2k/mo effective minimum | High | Immediate testing | Scaling what works organically |
| Partnerships (affiliates, integrations, bundles) | Commission or free license | Medium | Months | Distribution leverage |
| App/store placement (Product Hunt, AlternativeTo, app store feature) | \$0 | Low | Low | Launch spikes |

## Channel scoring framework

Score each channel on five dimensions (1–5 scale). Weight dimensions by your current goal.

| Dimension | What it measures |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Audience fit | Does this channel reach the target segment? |
| Effort per customer | Time and content needed to realize the same impact; lower is better. |
| Cost efficiency | Customer acquisition cost (CAC) — both monetary and time |
| Competitor noise | How many competing messages flood the channel? Higher is worse |
| Longevity | If you pause the channel, does traffic cease? A channel with longevity builds compound value. |

### Example: Focus Forge scorecard

| Channel | Audience fit | Effort | CAC | Noise | Longevity | Total |
|---------|-------------|--------|-----|-------|-----------|-------|
| Email / newsletter | 4 | 3 | 3 | 2 | 5 | 17 |
| Hacker News | 4 | 3 | 2 | 4 | 2 | 15 |
| Reddit (r/productivity, etc.) | 4 | 4 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 17 |
| Twitter (build-in-public) | 4 | 3 | 2 | 4 | 3 | 16 |
| Product Hunt launch | 3 | 1 (one-time event) | 4 | 4 | 2 | 14 |
| Blog / SEO | 4 | 1 (takes months) | 4 | 4 | 5 | 18 |

Blog/SEO ranks highest on longevity but is the worst for a solo product launch in a new category. Email list building and community (Reddit) emerge early at the correct mix for speed + growth.

## The owned-earned-paid flywheel

The model that most small teams should build an engine for:

1. **Owned** → create something useful (blog post, tool, video)
2. **Earned** → the content gets shared and mentioned by others (upvotes, retweets, links)
3. **Paid** → amplify what is already working (boost the top performing piece)

Never skip: owned content right before a paid push. Without organic proof of yourself, paid is responsive but not performed.

## Sequencing channels for a launch

| Period | Active channels | Focus |
|--------|----------------|-------|
| Pre-launch (Weeks 1–4) | Blog, Twitter, email waitlist | Build audiance, share snippets, collect emails |
| Launch week | All owned + Hacker News + Product Hunt + community posts | Maximum concentrated burst |
| Month 1 post-launch | Email, follow-up content, paid test | Turn launch visitors into buyers |
| Months 2–3 | Blog, SEO, organic in communities | Establish sustainability |

### Volume expectations for a solo founder

A single (no ad spend) launch can produce:

- Email waitlist: 100–500 signups over 4–6 weeks
- Launch day traffic: 1k–10k visits
- Launch week conversions: 15–60 sales at \$15
- Post-launch: 100–400 visitors/day from organic and community if consistent

These numbers are optimistic for a well-planned no-ads launch. Plan the campaign for these outcomes, not for virus growth.

## Launch vs. sustainable channels

A launch gets you to first revenue. Sustainability gets you to consistency.

|  | Launch channel | Interim (sustained) channel |
|---|---|---|
| Hacker News / Product Hunt | One big push | Occasional (or platform burn) |
| Community (Reddit, Reader group) | Activated early | Steady contribution / participation |
| Email newsletter | Pre-build list | Per-new-post content |
| Blog | Pre-launch teasers | Post-launch cadence |

## Channel development checklist

- [ ] For each channel you intend to use, write the name of the specific community (Reddit handle, podcast name, blog name)
- [ ] How many hours per week person will the sustained channel cost? If more than one channel combined costs >10 hours, two is max for a solo founder
- [ ] What is the RCI (cost per expected conversion) for the paid channel? Imagine the budget across two different paid channels
- [ ] How does the channel handle failure? An owned channel (email list) still exists after no one opens the first email; a social channel may not
- [ ] Which channel should you start week 1 — wait, not launch week? The answer is almost always email newsletter or content seeding before launch.

## Competitor content reading

- **channels that are required:** Yes, Product Hunt review, Hacker News. For a $15 target for indie/productivity product, those are almost certain to drive most at-traffic on launch.
- **channels to skip:** TV, radio, billboards, premium print, SEO through brand. These cost more than the revenue of a $15 per-product app.
- **channels to keep small:** Podcast sponsors (too expensive for solo founders), LinkedIn (sagging for B2C productivity), TikTok (audience might be too young).
