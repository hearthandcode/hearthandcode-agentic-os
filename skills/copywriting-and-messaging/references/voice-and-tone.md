# Voice and Tone

## Voice Attributes

Voice does not change. It is the brand's personality, the same across every piece.

Define a voice in 3–5 attributes. Each attribute should come with a plain-language meaning and example.

**Example voice architecture:**

| Attribute | Meaning | Do this | Avoid this |
|-----------|---------|---------|------------|
| Direct | Say what matters in the first five words | Start every CTA with a verb | burying the point in context |
| Helpful | Anticipate the reader's next question | End each section with what they will want next | leaving them hunting for information |
| Confident | Make a claim without hedging | "This works." | "We believe that" or "try to offer" |
| Human | Sound like a person, not a committee | Use contractions, x, short sentences | Jargon, first sentence clauses |

**Four rules that never change:**

1. **The reader decides what the message says.** What you intend is irrelevant; what they understand is all that matters. If a sentence is leaking, rewrite it.
2. **Shorter sentences work better.** Aim for average 22 words. If every sentence runs over 23, the rhythm breaks.
3. **Avoid jargon unless you are writing to experts.** "Optimize" = "do better," "onboard" = "get started," "leverage" = "use."
4. **One thought per paragraph.** A block of text that makes three points is three paragraphs.

## Tone Modulation

Tone shifts by context. The voice stays the same, but the formality and energy level change.

**Tone spectrum (low to high warmth, low to high energy):**

| Context | Tone | Example |
|---------|------|---------|
| Error message that costs time | Apologetic, direct | "You just lost your changes. Auto-save unsupported. Copy your work before leaving this page." |
| Confirmation screen | Warm, concise | "All set. Your order starts processing immediately." |
| Marketing campaign | Energetic, aspirational | "One week in Paris for the price of one flight." |
| FAQ / support doc | Neutral, precise | "Works on iOS 17 and later. For Android 13+, download the companion app." |

**Tone modulation checklist:**
- Is this a joy moment, a fear moment, or a neutral moment?
  - High: shorter sentences and more punctuation
  - Fear: clear guidance and reassurance > motivation
  - Neutral leave for less than six words
- Would this sentence sound wrong if read to a real person? If yes, change it.
- What would you not say out loud in conversation? Do not write it.

## Style Rules

### Numbers and units
- Write out one through nine; numerals from 10 and up.
- Always write percentages as numerals: 3%, 17%.
- Keep dates and times in unambiguous format: "June 14, 2023" not "14/6/03."

### Formality
- Contractions are allowed, even expected ("don't," "it's," "we're").
- Every contraction avoided sounds stiff. Use "cannot" only when it's important.

### Punctuation
- Use one space after a period, always.
- Use the Oxford comma. No excuses.
- Use em dashes for emphasis — they work better than parentheses for readability.
- Keep semicolons for the situations you e.g., needed them two more of them.

### Headlines and Titles
- Title case for page titles, article titles, proper product names.
- Sentence case for subheads, pull quotes, navigation labels.
- End of headline? No period. Unless it's a full sentence and it adds clarity.

### Voice do not
- "We are excited to announce" — always cut. The news speaks for itself.
- "Revolutionary" or "game-changing" — the reader will not believe it, and if it is true the numbers prove it.
- "I think," "personally," "in my opinion" — remove by default, except in bylined opinion pieces.
- "Just" — almost always a conversational filler that adds nothing to the sentence meaning.
