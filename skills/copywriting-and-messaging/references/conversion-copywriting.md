# Conversion Copywriting

## CTAs — Calls to Action

The CTA is the moment the reader decides. Everything before it is a lead-up.

**Three rules:**

1. **One CTA per viewport.** More than one button asking for different actions confuses the reader. Which action is more important? The short-brain browser will decide no action.
2. **Start with a verb.** "Start your free trial," "Download the report," "Get the template." Do not start with a noun or article. "The free trial:" is dead.
3. **Add specificity.** "Start your free trial" beats "Try it free" because it tells the user the behavior required and the experience following. Add time, authority levels, outcomes.

**CTA color and size:**
- One distinct button color for the primary action
- Secondary on the same page should be a label or outline style
- Do not make the CTA look like a banner or decoration

**Common CTA pitfalls and fixes:**
| Pitfall | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| "Learn More" | "See how [company] uses this to cut costs by X%." |
| "Submit" | "Get your results" |
| "Click Here" | "Start your 14-day trial" |
| Multiple comparable buttons | Rank one as primary (solid bg), others as text |

## Objection Handling

Every reader who has an objection will not buy. The objection is almost always one of: "This is not for my business," "I need commitment post," "Too expensive," "I don't trust you."

**Method — counter via framing:**
- Anticipate the objection in copy
- Acknowledge it factually
- Counter without being defensive

**Common objections, what they mean, and how to answer:**

- **"It looks expensive."** The reader does not know if the value takes into account the price. How to suggest: offer a small self-serve starting price or a money-back guarantee first.
- **"I do not have time."** The reader assumes it is high effort/investment time. Response: "This takes [measure: minutes] from your time."
- **"We tried something like this before."** The reader thinks the result will be the same. It is not about the category but about the difference with the failed experience.

**Objection reversal in the FAQ:** "[Cost]. That's more than [current solution]. True. On the other hand, you will cut [the %] from the current solution cost, because ..."

## Ethical Urgency

**What is ethical urgency:** A real time limit or a real scarcity that is communicated honestly. High-pressure composites like "only 4 units left!" on a SaaS product cannot be the main tactic.

**Forms of ethical urgency:**
- Limited-time launch pricing with two-step expiration (early adopters get more)
- Real inventory scarcity — "there are only 12 items actually in stock as of [date]"
- Deadline-based rates or bonus bundle that ends
- A waiting list that opens for the next cohort

**Never fake:** For "the urgency is fake, it seems low trust the position."

## Risk Reversal

The reader takes risk. Copy needs to reduce it. Pricing showing enough risk reversal can help. The difference between a prospect and a lead is risk assumed.

**Risk reversal mechanisms for copy:**
- Free trial
- Guarantee period
- "Cancel any time" text
- Free sample, PDF preview, free consultation
- "We'll do the first design for free — you can take the output if you do not upgrade."

**The strongest one-liner:** "Start with a free release. If you cancel within 30 days, you keep the content. Only what works costs you."

## Formatting for conversion

- **Readability trump:** 12-14 lines on a very dark 85px (serif for body text)
- **Blank space:** 40, not 30%
- **Make text skimmable:** Clear subheads, bullet lists at decision points, bold terms that the skimmer will stop at

## Before and after conversion checks

**A landing page that does not convert:**
- The page does not ask for conversion up to the bottom
- The CTA is buried
- The page is a feature dump
- No social proof nor risk reversal

**A landing page that converts:**
- One clear appropriate CTA for the reader
- the primary benefit in the headline
- trust signals near the CTA (guarantee, transparent pricing, testimonials)
- copied section: simple design
