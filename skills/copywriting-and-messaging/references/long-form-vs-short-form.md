# Long Form vs Short Form

## When Short Form Wins

Short form works when the decision is low-cost and the reader's intent is high: a buy sign, a subscription, a repeat search they already know.

**Characteristics of good short-form territory:**
- The reader is already 80% of the way to a decision.
- The price is below whatever point requires elaboration (for B2C, typically under $100).
- The value proposition is self-evident — "automates invoicing" for a freelancer.
- The medium limits attention — trade ad, social feed, email subject line, billboard.

**Short-form page structure:**
1. Headline (one line)
2. Subhead (one sentence of elaboration)
3. 2-3 benefit sentences
4. One image or diagram
5. CTA

**Danger:** The reader who needs convincing will skip. Short-form is for catcher of browsers, not converter of skeptics.

---

## When Long Form Wins

Long form works when the product is expensive, the decision is complex, or the reader needs to overcome skepticism.

**Key territory:**
- Price > $200 or monthly commitment > $50.
- The product requires explanation (a three-step process, a new category).
- The reader is actively comparing competitors and will read.
- The market is saturated, and trust is the success factor.

**The "three-persuasions" rule of long form:**
Three-tier convincing: emotional connection ("I want this"), logical argument ("this makes sense"), evidence ("others confirm").

**Long-form page structure:**
1. Header with 25 support subhead
2. Problem section ("Here is the situation that is painful")
3. Solution section ("We fix it")
4. How it works (3 steps max, each 2-4 sentences)
5. Full benefits section (6-12 benefits, each with feature → benefit → "so what?")
6. Social proof (testimonials, case studies, news — at least 3 pieces)
7. Objection handling (FAQs, guarantees, risk reversal)
8. CTA with recap and urgency (re-states the offer + next step)

## Hybrid: Modular pages

The truest pattern is not short or long: it is modular. Write each block so it is 5 lines or less under a heading. The scroll-depth decides the order. A heavy short block may push long readers down and short ones off — so you design two block priorities: the first few are above the fold, the rest support the skimmer to go deeper.

**The version:**
- The top: short form style — 3-5 lines, CTA always visible.
- Mid-page: expansions for who reads further (testimonials, how it works walk-through).
- Bottom: The deep defenders (FAQs, full specification, pricing details.)
- CTA repeat: in the bottom, after the bottom row.

---

## Structural rules (both forms)

1. **The first 50 words must confirm or reverse the headline.** No gentle introduction. The reader already made a decision to stay or go: to that moment.
2. **End every paragraph with the sense of next step.** Every paragraph ends in a hook that lands the next sentence. It can be a concept, a contrast, or a question.
3. **Use short paragraphs — 3-4 sentences each — even in long form.** Reading on screen is slower unless the text is broken.
4. **Read a sentence aloud. If it sounds odd, edit it.**
5. **Each section should be independent.** A reader who picks up from the middle should still understand the argument.
