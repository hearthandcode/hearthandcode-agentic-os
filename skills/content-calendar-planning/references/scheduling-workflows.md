# Scheduling Workflows

Role: methodology. Governs batching, tool-agnostic pipelines, and approvals — how
planned slots become scheduled, published assets with the least weekly friction.

## The batching principle

Production runs in batches, not in daily scraps of attention. Context switching is
the hidden cost: writing one essay across five evenings costs far more than one
protected block. The calendar's weekly unit is the batch.

## The weekly batch block

- Reserve one recurring block per week — 2-4 hours, same day, defended like a client
  meeting. Scattered minutes do not survive real weeks.
- Sequence the block: outline first (cheap, unblock everything), then draft, then
  edit, then schedule. Never start a batch by polishing.
- Draft next week's assets in this week's batch — a standing one-batch buffer is the
  difference between a missed slot and an invisible slip.
- End every batch with the queue loaded and a one-line note of what next week's
  batch must start on.

## The production pipeline

Every asset moves through the same five stages:

1. **Outline** — one page: claim, structure, example, call to action.
2. **Draft** — written fast, no editing mid-draft.
3. **Edit** — two passes minimum: structure pass, then line pass.
4. **Asset prep** — graphics, links, alt text, previews.
5. **Schedule** — loaded into the publishing tool with date, time, and surface.

Track the stage per slot in the calendar (the slot status vocabulary from
calendar fundamentals). The pipeline and the statuses must agree; if the grid says
`drafted` but no draft exists, the grid is lying.

## Tool-agnostic pipeline

Any tool stack must cover four functions; the specific product does not matter:

| Function | What it must do | Typical answers |
|---|---|---|
| Calendar of record | Show slots, statuses, dates in one view | Spreadsheet, board tool, calendar app |
| Drafting | Version drafts without email attachments | Any editor with files, notes app |
| Scheduling | Post at a set time without you present | Native schedulers, social tools, newsletter platform |
| Review capture | Store feedback next to the asset | Comments in the draft doc, a review column |

Rules for the stack:

- One calendar of record. Mirror copies go stale within a month.
- Native scheduling tools beat universal dashboards on reliability per surface.
- If a tool needs more upkeep than the content, drop the tool.
- Export the calendar of record monthly; tools change, the plan outlives them.

## Approvals and review

Solo creators skip approvals by default — but three situations still need a
human-in-the-loop step:

1. **Anything with other people's names, quotes, or data in it** — they see it
   before it ships.
2. **Paid or promotional content** — one cooling-off pass before scheduling.
3. **Anything you would hesitate to show a client** — that hesitation is the review
   signal; sleep on it once.

For collaborations, the review loop is contractual: agree on a review deadline at
booking time (default: 5 business days before publish), and hold the right to pull
the slot if review slips past it. A guest asset that misses its review date costs
its slot, not its quality bar.

## Publish-day mechanics

- Schedule in the surface's native tool the day the asset clears its quality floor.
- Verify the scheduled queue weekly — time zones and re-edits are where scheduled
  posts silently break.
- After publishing, mark the slot `published` in the same sitting; a calendar that
  drifts from reality stops being usable within two weeks.

## Anti-patterns

- Batching that is actually five sessions of "checking the draft."
- A second tracking tool because the first felt stale — reconcile or delete instead.
- Scheduling assets that never cleared the quality floor because the slot is close.
- Approval loops for solo content nobody requested them for; friction without a
  stakeholder is just delay.
