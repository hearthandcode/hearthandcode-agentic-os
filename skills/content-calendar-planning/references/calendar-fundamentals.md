# Calendar Fundamentals

Role: methodology. This file governs how a content calendar is structured — planning
horizons, the difference between slots and ideas, and the cadence math that keeps a
calendar publishable by a real person with a real week.

## Why a calendar is a capacity instrument

A content calendar is not a list of ideas. It is a promise schedule: it maps the
content you intend to publish onto the time you actually have to make it. Every rule
in this file exists to keep that promise honest. A calendar built on ambition fails in
week two, and a calendar that fails repeatedly teaches the audience that the schedule
means nothing.

## The three horizons

| Horizon | Span | Resolved at | Owned artifact |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quarterly arc | 12-13 weeks | Theme level | Narrative arc, launch windows, seasonal peaks |
| Monthly grid | 4-5 weeks | Slot level | The working calendar with slot statuses |
| Weekly batch | 1 week | Asset level | Batching list with working titles |

Layering rules:

1. Decide the quarterly theme before filling any monthly slot, or the grid drifts.
2. Fill monthly slots at pillar-and-format level before writing titles.
3. Write titles and outlines in the weekly batch — never more than two weeks ahead.
4. A quarterly theme changes only at a quarterly review, not mid-month because one
   post underperformed.

## Slots versus ideas

A slot is a reserved position on the calendar with four properties: platform, format,
date, and status. An idea is unplaced potential living in a backlog.

- The calendar is full when slots have statuses, not when the idea list is long.
- Moving an idea into a slot is a capacity decision, not a creative one. Every idea
  either earns a slot or stays parked.
- Slot status vocabulary: `empty` → `outlined` → `drafted` → `scheduled` →
  `published` → `reviewed`.
- The grid records status per slot. An asset with no slot is not on the calendar; a
  slot with no asset is a vacancy to manage, not a shame to hide.

## Sustainable cadence math

Compute cadence from measured capacity, never from ambition:

1. Measure batching hours per week (B): the hours you will actually spend producing.
   Measure two real weeks before trusting any number.
2. Measure hours per asset (H) for each format: essay, long post, short post, video.
3. Allocate B across formats in priority order: assets per format = B share / H.
4. Subtract a safety margin — one asset, or 20 percent of B, whichever is smaller —
   before committing the calendar.

Worked micro-example: B = 6 hours. Newsletter essay H = 3.0, short post H = 0.5.
Six hours buys 1 essay (3h) + 6 short posts (3h). Apply the margin: commit to
1 essay + 4-5 cuts, hold the remaining hour as flex.

A cadence you cannot hold for four consecutive weeks is not a cadence; it is a
sprint. Sprints end in silence.

## Capacity audit before commitment

- List recurring obligations that compete with production hours, including the
  invisible ones (admin, invoicing, email).
- Track actual production time for two weeks; almost everyone overestimates by
  30-50 percent.
- Identify the batching window that survives a bad week — usually one protected
  block, not scattered minutes.
- Record hours-per-asset per format from real drafts, not estimates.

## Fill order when capacity is scarce

1. Anchor slots first — the flagship asset (newsletter, video, show) that other
   assets derive from.
2. Derivative cuts second — repurposed versions of the anchor.
3. Relationship slots third — replies, community prompts, collaborations.
4. Experimental slots last — at most one per month, clearly labeled as an experiment.

## Vacancy handling

When a slot cannot be filled by its publish date, choose exactly one:

- **Demote** — swap in a smaller format you can produce in the remaining time.
- **Resurface** — update and re-share a proven evergreen asset with a fresh angle.
- **Cut** — skip the slot silently. No apology post, no filler.

Never publish filler to protect the schedule. One filler post costs more trust than
one missed slot, because the audience can tell and you can too.

## Anti-patterns

- Planning a month of daily posts without measuring a single real production week.
- Treating the idea backlog as capacity ("I have 40 ideas, so I can post daily").
- Copying another creator's cadence without their batching setup or team size.
- Filling every slot at 100 percent of measured capacity with zero margin.
- Letting the quarterly theme mutate mid-month under single-post pressure.
