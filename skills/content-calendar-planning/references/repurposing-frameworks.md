# Repurposing Frameworks

Role: methodology. Governs the pillar-to-atom model and the discipline of cutting
one anchor asset into many platform-native pieces without flooding the audience.

## Why repurposing is structural, not optional

A solo creator cannot out-produce a team, but a team's output per idea is far lower
than a solo operator's can be. Repurposing is the multiplier: one well-built anchor
essay should yield a week or more of derivative assets. Design cuts into the calendar
when slots are assigned — never as an afterthought on publish day.

## The pillar-to-atom model

Every asset exists at one of two levels:

- **Pillar asset (anchor):** the long-form unit — newsletter essay, long video,
  webinar, guide. It carries the full argument, examples, and evidence.
- **Atom asset (cut):** a self-contained piece extracted from the anchor — a claim,
  a number, a story, a framework, a quote, a contrarian take. It must stand alone
  with no "read the full essay" crutch in its first line.

## The standard cut list

One anchor essay yields a default set of cuts:

1. **The thesis post** — the essay's core claim in 2-3 sentences.
2. **The list thread** — the essay's structure as 5-8 numbered posts.
3. **The number post** — the single most surprising statistic or measurement,
   with its context.
4. **The story post** — the anecdote that opens the essay, told in first person.
5. **The contrarian take** — the essay's most disputed claim, sharpened.
6. **The framework graphic or text diagram** — the essay's core model, visualized.
7. **The Q&A post** — the question the essay answers, posted alone; replies carry
   the short answer and link nothing.

Not every essay produces all seven. Produce the 3-5 cuts that map to the essay's
actual strongest material — a data-light opinion essay has no number post.

## Platform-native rule

A cut is rewritten for the surface, not copied:

- Short-post surfaces want a hook in the first line and no more than one idea.
- Professional feeds want the takeaway up top, then the supporting detail.
- Threads want a first post that works as a standalone post.
- Never paste essay paragraphs into any surface; the compression IS the rewrite.

## Spacing and frequency rules

- Space cuts of the same anchor at least 2-3 days apart on the same surface.
- A maximum of one cut per surface per day, even across different anchors.
- Rotate cut angles within the week: thesis Monday, story Wednesday, number Friday.
  Two cuts with the same angle in one week read as repetition, not reinforcement.
- If performance of cut N materially drops versus cut 1, the audience has absorbed
  the idea — stop cutting that essay and start the next anchor.

## Building cuts into the grid

When assigning the anchor's slot, immediately assign its cuts as their own slots
with `source: anchor-id` recorded in the slot:

- The cut inherits the anchor's pillar; the mix grid counts it there.
- If the anchor slips, its cuts slip with it — never publish cuts before the anchor.
- A cut slot left empty when the anchor published is a vacancy, not a savings.

## The repurposing log

Keep a lightweight table: anchor id, title, cuts produced, cut slots used, cuts
skipped with a one-word reason (weak material / audience fatigue / angle already
used). Monthly review of the log shows which pillars produce cuttable anchors and
which produce only dead ends — a pillar that never yields a second strong cut is
thin, whatever its traffic says.

## Anti-patterns

- Publishing all seven cuts of one essay across three days on the same surface —
  that is a feed takeover, not a strategy.
- Cuts that make sense only if you read the essay first.
- Treating "repurposing" as cross-posting identical text to multiple surfaces.
- Cutting weak anchors out of obligation; obligation cuts are the fastest route to
  audience fatigue with the whole pillar.
