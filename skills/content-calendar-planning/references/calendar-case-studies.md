# Calendar Case Studies

Role: case study. A complete, fictional worked month for one solo creator — built,
executed, and reviewed — showing the skill's method under realistic constraints.
All names, numbers, and outcomes are invented for teaching purposes.

## The creator

**Maya** runs a newsletter, *The Slow Build* (weekly, Thursdays), about durable
software practices for solo developers, plus two surfaces: a short-post platform
and a professional feed. She works alone, bills client hours, and has previously
tried and abandoned a "post daily" plan — it lasted nine days.

## Step 1 — Capacity audit

Maya measures two real weeks before committing anything:

- Newsletter essay: 3.5 hours (draft 2h, edits 1h, links/prep 0.5h)
- Short post: 0.5 hours; thread: 1.5 hours
- Professional feed post: 0.75 hours
- Realistic batching hours: 6 per week (Tuesday morning block, 2h; Thursday
  afternoon, 2h; Friday wrap, 2h)

Cadence math (per the fundamentals file): 6 hours = 1 essay (3.5h) + 5 hours
remaining → 4 short posts (2h) + 3 feed posts (2.25h) = 7.75h — over budget. She
applies the safety margin and commits to:

- 1 essay + 3 cuts per week (short-post platform: 2, feed: 1, derived from the essay)
- 1 independent short post per week (craft observations from client work)
- Weekly total: 3.5 + 1.5 (3 cuts) + 0.5 = 5.5h — inside 6h with margin.

## Step 2 — Pillars and ratios

Maya's pillars and monthly targets for 12 slots:

| Pillar | Target | Slots |
|---|---|---|
| Durable practices (core craft) | 50% | 6 |
| Solo business reality (proof/stories) | 33% | 4 |
| Tooling opinions (perspective) | 17% | 2 |

Her named series: **"Anatomy of a Rewrite"** — a 4-part series where each newsletter
essay dissects one stage of rewriting a real (fictional, anonymized) side project.
Episodes 1-2 published in prior weeks; this month carries episodes 3-4 and the
finale recap.

## Step 3 — The four-week grid (slot level)

Week 1: Essay (series ep. 3) · 2 cuts · 1 independent short post
Week 2: Essay (series ep. 4) · 2 cuts · 1 feed post (proof angle)
Week 3: Essay (series finale recap) · 2 cuts · 1 independent short post
Week 4: Recovery-adjacent week — 1 evergreen essay refresh (banked asset) · 1 cut ·
community reply slot

Total: 12 slots, mix lands at 50/33/17 as targeted.

## Step 4 — Execution log (what actually happened)

- Week 1: all slots published. The number-cut ("3 rewrites taught me 80% of graders
  don't check edge cases") over-performed the account's recent average by roughly
  3x in replies. Surprise of the week.
- Week 2: essay published Thursday. One cut slipped to Monday — the batch ran long
  editing the essay, and the cut was deprioritized rather than rushed below its
  floor. Logged, not apologized for.
- Week 3: finale recap published. Cuts fine. Independent short post under-performed;
  Maya's note: "client anecdote needed the essay treatment — too compressed."
- Week 4: banked refresh published; community reply slot used to answer the three
  best questions the series raised. Zero new production hours — by design.

## Step 5 — The monthly review (keep/kill/iterate)

- **Keep:** the series format (episodes 3-4 held attention; finale drove the
  month's best subscriber day), the Tuesday/Thursday/Friday batch structure.
- **Iterate:** the number-cut angle worked; next month Maya pre-plans one number
  cut per essay instead of choosing cuts on Friday. One variable changed.
- **Kill:** none — but the independent short post gets one more month on probation,
  with a recorded reason: "two of four underperformed; needs better material
  selection, not format death."

## What made it work

1. Cadence was computed from measured hours, not ambition — the plan that failed at
   nine days was replaced by one that fit 5.5 of 6 available hours.
2. The series carried recognition; cuts carried reach; the roles never blurred.
3. The one slip was absorbed by the buffer (cut moved, nothing rushed, nothing
   apologized for).
4. The recovery-adjacent week 4 was planned before the month started, not improvised
   after burnout.
5. The review changed exactly one thing — so next month's results stay readable.

## The counter-case (what not to do)

Maya's earlier attempt, reconstructed: 14 slots planned for week 1 alone, no
measured hours, cuts invented on publish day, no review day. It collapsed in nine
days and — worse — trained her audience that announcements of new schedules were
meaningless. The rebuilt calendar's first rule was capacity honesty; everything else
in this skill exists to protect that rule.
