# Performance Review Loop

Role: methodology. Governs the weekly review, keep/kill/iterate decisions, and how
performance evidence flows back into the next calendar without overcorrecting.

## The loop in one sentence

Publish on plan, review on a fixed day with fixed questions, and let the calendar —
not mood or one viral post — absorb the conclusions.

## The weekly review

Fixed day, fixed 30 minutes, fixed questions. Same day every week, attached to the
batch block's eve so findings land directly in the next batch.

The five questions:

1. **Shipped?** Which planned slots published, which slipped, and why (one line).
2. **What moved?** For each published asset: the one number that matters for its
   job (opens and replies for newsletter; engaged replies and saves for social —
   not raw impressions).
3. **What surprised?** Any asset that meaningfully over- or under-performed its
   format's recent average. Surprise is the signal; the rest is noise.
4. **Cost check?** Did batching stay inside its hours, or did production bleed?
5. **Next week's one change?** Exactly one adjustment, written into the next grid
   before the review ends.

## Keep / kill / iterate

Classify each format, series, pillar, and slot type monthly — not weekly:

- **Keep:** performed at or above its job's bar, and cost inside budget. No change.
- **Iterate:** the idea works, the execution drags. Change one variable — format,
  timing, angle, length — never several at once, or the next result is unreadable.
- **Kill:** missed its bar for 3 consecutive attempts, or its cost exceeded its
  contribution for a full month. Kill means the recurring slot leaves the template.
  Individual assets are never "killed" — only recurring patterns are.

Decision hygiene:

- One-line written reason for every kill, kept with the log. Kills made without
  recorded reasons get re-litigated forever.
- A kill frees a slot; assign the freed slot in the same session or it refills
  itself by default with whatever is easiest to make.
- Never kill a series mid-arc without an ending slot; end it, do not amputate it.

## Metrics that match the job

Match the metric to the asset's purpose before judging it:

| Asset job | Primary metric | Wrong metric |
|---|---|---|
| Newsletter essay | Open rate, replies | Follower growth |
| Reach post (thread, short post) | Profile visits, follows | Saves |
| Proof asset (case, teardown) | Saves, shares, inbound questions | Impressions |
| Community post | Replies, DMs started | Reach |

An asset can succeed at its job while looking weak on the wrong metric — judging
reach posts by saves kills exactly the posts that grow the audience.

## Feeding back without overcorrecting

- Weekly reviews change one thing; monthly reviews change the mix.
- Never change pillar ratios on a single week's data — a pillar needs a month of
  slots before its ratio is judgeable.
- Distinguish audience signal from platform noise: distribution swings for reasons
  unrelated to quality. If a drop is unexplained, hold the plan and re-check next
  week before reacting.
- Keep a running one-line-per-week diary next to the grid. Three lines of context
  prevent next quarter's "why did we cut Thursdays?" archaeology.

## The review artifact

Keep the weekly review to a fixed small shape — date, shipped/slipped, surprise of
the week, the one change — stored alongside the calendar of record. The artifact
matters because the loop compounds: eight weeks of reviews is a strategy document
written by evidence instead of opinion.

## Anti-patterns

- Reviewing daily; daily numbers move for noise reasons and burn the discipline the
  loop depends on.
- The "one change" quietly becoming five changes every week.
- Metrics copied from another creator with a different business model (subscriber
  counts as a proxy for a relationship-driven newsletter).
- Reviews that produce admiration and no calendar edits. A review that changes
  nothing was a report, not a review.
