# Content Mix Design

Role: methodology. Governs pillar ratios, format rotation, and series slots — what
the calendar is made of, as opposed to how often it publishes.

## Pillars and ratios

A pillar is a recurring theme your audience associates with you. Three to five
pillars maximum for a solo creator; more than that dilutes recognition and makes the
mix unmeasurable.

Assign each pillar a target ratio — its intended share of monthly slots:

- Ratios are set by strategy (what you want to be known for), then checked against
  performance data — never the reverse. Performance tunes the ratio; it does not
  define the pillars.
- A practical starting shape for a practitioner brand: 50 percent core craft (your
  main competence), 25 percent proof (cases, teardowns, worked examples),
  15 percent perspective (opinion, story), 10 percent community (answers, prompts,
  shares).
- Check the realized ratio monthly: count published slots per pillar, compare to
  targets, and correct by allocating slots — not by rewriting the strategy.

## Format rotation

Formats fail differently: essays build depth, short posts build frequency, threads
build reach, video builds trust. Rotation prevents the calendar from becoming one
trick that decays invisibly.

- Rotate at least two formats per pillar per month.
- Match format to pillar intent: proof material carries best as a case study or
  teardown thread; perspective carries best as an essay or story post.
- Keep one default format per surface so production stays efficient
  (newsletter = essay, short-post platform = short post with occasional thread).
- Introduce a new format as a labeled experiment in a slot you can afford to lose.

## Series slots

A series is a named, numbered, repeating slot. It converts sporadic posting into an
appointment and gives cuts from any pillar a predictable home.

- Run at most two named series at once as a solo creator.
- A series earns its slot after three episodes that hold attention; before that it
  is an experiment and is labeled as one.
- End a series deliberately — a finale, not a fade. A series that decays into random
  gaps teaches the audience to ignore announcements.
- Number the episodes publicly; the number is the appointment signal.

## The mix grid

Lay the month as pillar (rows) by format (columns). Each published slot lands in one
cell. A healthy month has no empty rows and no cell holding more than a third of the
month's slots.

Worked micro-example — 12 monthly slots, pillars Craft / Proof / Perspective:

| Pillar | Essay | Thread | Short post | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Craft | 2 | 1 | 3 | 6 |
| Proof | 1 | 1 | 0 | 2 |
| Perspective | 1 | 0 | 3 | 4 |

Realized ratio: 50 / 17 / 33. Perspective is overweight because it is the easiest to
write — a classic drift. Next month's grid trims one perspective short post and adds
one proof case.

## Sequencing rules

1. Proof follows craft: publish the framework, then its worked example within two
   weeks, while the idea is still warm.
2. Never schedule two heavyweight slots (essay + long video) on consecutive days;
   the second inherits the first's fatigue.
3. Give each anchor a clear runway: no competing anchor on the same surface within
   48 hours.
4. Community slots orbit anchors — a reader question lands after the post that
   provokes it.

## Balance checks

Run these monthly against the grid:

- No pillar at zero: silence is how a pillar dies.
- No single cell above a third of the month: one-format monocultures decay quietly.
- Every pillar has at least one asset you were slightly nervous to publish; an
  all-safe month predicts a flat month.

## Anti-patterns

- Pillar ratios copied from a creator with a different business model.
- A grid that is 80 percent one pillar because that pillar is easiest to write.
- Launching series faster than they can be sustained; three stalled series train
  the audience to ignore every announcement.
- Changing the mix mid-month on the strength of one post's numbers.
