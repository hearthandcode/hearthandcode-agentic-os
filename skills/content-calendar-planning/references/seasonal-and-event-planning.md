# Seasonal and Event Planning

Role: checklist. Governs launch arcs, holiday planning, and lead times — the
date-driven layer that sits on top of the normal calendar. Work through every
applicable checklist when a launch, holiday, or industry event touches the planning
window.

## Launch arc checklist (four weeks out to post-launch)

- [ ] Four weeks out: define the launch goal and the single audience action
      (subscribe, register, buy, reply).
- [ ] Four weeks out: block the launch slot and the two slots before it in the grid
      before anything else claims them.
- [ ] Three weeks out: outline the warm-up assets — problem posts, backstory posts,
      the "why now" post.
- [ ] Two weeks out: draft the launch asset itself; book any collaboration or guest
      slots in writing.
- [ ] One week out: confirm every asset in the arc is drafted and past its quality
      floor; schedule the launch-day sequence.
- [ ] Launch day: the anchor asset plus at most one short post per surface. Resist
      the five-post blast; it cannibalizes itself.
- [ ] Week after: publish the retrospective or results post; thank collaborators
      publicly and specifically.

## Holiday and seasonal checklist

- [ ] List the holidays that matter to THIS audience — not all holidays.
- [ ] Decide a stance per holiday: theme a post, reduce cadence, or ignore entirely.
      Write the decision into the grid; undecided holidays become vacancies.
- [ ] Plan reduced-cadence windows in the grid before filling slots, so the month
      absorbs the gap by design instead of by failure.
- [ ] Prepare banked evergreen assets for high-distraction weeks instead of forcing
      fresh flagship work into them.
- [ ] Check whether the audience's fiscal or academic calendar (year-end budgets,
      semester starts, conference season) creates attention windows that matter more
      than public holidays.

## Lead-time table

| Asset type | Minimum lead time | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Short post | 2-3 days | Fits inside one weekly batch |
| Newsletter essay | 1 week | Draft plus two edit passes |
| Collaboration or guest slot | 3-4 weeks | Runs on another person's calendar, not yours |
| Long video | 2-3 weeks | Script, shoot, and edit are separate cycles |
| Product launch arc | 6-8 weeks | Warm-up, launch, and follow-up each need real slots |

Lead time is measured to the publish date — including review, edits, and
scheduling — not to the first draft.

## Event-driven content rules

1. React to industry news only when you add what the coverage lacks: a mechanism, a
   number, a worked example. Same-week reaction or skip entirely.
2. Cap reactive content at one slot in five. A calendar of reactions has no moat
   and no archive value.
3. For planned industry events (conferences, product releases), pre-draft the
   reaction skeleton so event day is editing, not writing from zero.
4. Never promise coverage of an event before confirming you can attend, watch, or
   license it. Broken coverage promises outrank broken posting streaks.

## Post-event and post-launch hygiene

- Within one week: log what the arc produced — subscribes, replies, sign-ups — next
  to the slots that produced it.
- Within two weeks: decide which launch assets become evergreen, refresh them, and
  re-slot them in the next quarter.
- Retire the arc's slots from the template so the next month starts clean instead
  of dragging launch scaffolding forward.

## Common seasonal failures

- Deciding on December 20th that December is quiet, after the grid was full.
- Themed holiday posts nobody on the audience calendar asked for.
- Launch arcs with warm-up and launch but no follow-through slot — the week after
  launch is where momentum is either kept or silently lost.
- Guest and collaboration slots treated as flexible; they are the only slots with
  another person's reputation attached.
