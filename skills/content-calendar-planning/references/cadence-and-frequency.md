# Cadence and Frequency

Role: methodology. Governs platform norms, quality floors, and recovery weeks — the
decision rules for how often to publish on each surface without degrading quality.

## Cadence is per-surface, not per-person

Each surface has its own consumption rhythm, production cost, and failure mode. Set
cadence per surface, then check the sum against total measured capacity. A single
global "how often do I post" number hides the trade that actually matters: which
surface earns its hours.

## Platform norms (directional starting points)

| Surface | Sustainable solo cadence | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Newsletter | Weekly or biweekly | Anchors the week; two consecutive misses measurably dents open rates |
| Short-post platform | 3-7 posts per week | A thread counts as one unit plus same-day follow-ups |
| Professional feed | 2-4 posts per week | Reach collapses on low-effort reposts; fewer, better |
| Long video | 1-2 per month | High production cost; batch twice monthly |
| Short video | 2-5 per week | Cheap only when filmed in batches, never daily |

These are directional starting points for a solo operator, not laws, and platform
distribution shifts over time. Your measured quality floor and your own performance
data outrank any table, including this one.

## Quality floors

Every format gets a written floor — the minimum bar an asset must clear to publish:

- **Newsletter essay:** one original idea or synthesis, one concrete example, two
  edit passes. If you cannot name the idea in one sentence, it is not drafted yet.
- **Short post:** one specific claim, no filler hashtags, readable in under 20
  seconds.
- **Thread:** a first line that would survive alone as a post, and a payoff in the
  final post — not a cliffhanger to nowhere.
- **Video:** a scripted outline, watchable audio, one clear takeaway inside the
  first 15 seconds.
- **Professional feed post:** the takeaway in the first two lines, before the fold.

If an asset misses its floor, it does not ship. Demote it, fix it in the next batch,
or cut it. Shipping below the floor trains the audience to expect below-the-floor.

## Frequency ceilings

Overpublishing damages a calendar as reliably as underpublishing:

- More than one anchor essay per week usually halves the per-essay effort and the
  quality with it.
- More than 8 short posts per week on one account burns your best observations in
  low-reach slots and reads as noise.
- Hours spent above the ceiling are stolen from next week's batch — the debt always
  arrives.

## Recovery weeks

Schedule a recovery week every 6-8 weeks of sustained publishing:

1. Pre-build it: during normal weeks, bank one or two evergreen assets.
2. During recovery, publish only banked assets and cut total slots roughly in half.
3. Announce nothing. A recovery week that requires an announcement is a backlog
   failure, not a rest week.
4. Return week runs the normal cadence; do not "make up" the missed slots.

## Missing a slot

- One miss: skip silently, resume at the next slot. No apology post.
- Two consecutive misses on the same surface: cut that surface's cadence by one unit
  per week. The cadence was miscalibrated, not your discipline.
- Never post a public apology for a missed slot. The audience noticed far less than
  you fear, and the apology converts a private miss into a public one.

## Signals your cadence is wrong

- Drafts routinely slip past their scheduled dates.
- You publish at the quality floor every week with no headroom.
- Batching bleeds into the weekend you reserved for recovery.
- Open rates or engagement trend down while output holds or rises.

Corrective move: cut one recurring slot for four weeks and remeasure. Cutting is a
calibration tool, not a failure — the calendar exists to serve sustainable output,
not the reverse.

## Anti-patterns

- Daily posting commitments made on a Monday that felt unusually free.
- Quality floors waived "just this week" more than once a quarter.
- Recovery weeks skipped because the bank is empty — that is the signal to schedule
  one immediately, not to push through.
- Treating a platform's peak-posting-time folklore as more important than your own
  audience's measured response.
