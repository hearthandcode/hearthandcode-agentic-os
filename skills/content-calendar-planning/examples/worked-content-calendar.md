# Worked Content Calendar — Code & Canvas

A solo creator. Weekly newsletter (Thursday) plus two surfaces: short-post platform
and professional feed. Deliverable: a 4-week calendar built from measured capacity,
pillar ratios, series slots, mapped repurposing cuts, a batching workflow, and one
completed weekly review. All names, numbers, and outcomes are fictional.

## 1. Capacity audit (measured, two real weeks)

| Format | Hours per asset |
|---|---|
| Newsletter essay | 3.0 (draft 2h, edit 0.75h, links/prep 0.25h) |
| Short post | 0.4 |
| Thread | 1.2 |
| Professional feed post | 0.6 |

Realistic batching hours: 6/week — Monday evening 2h (outlines), Wednesday 2h
(draft + edit), Friday 1h (cuts + scheduling), 1h held as reserve.

## 2. Cadence math

- 6h buys: 1 essay (3.0) + 3 cuts (2 × 0.4 short + 1 × 0.6 feed = 1.4) +
  1 independent short post (0.4) = 4.8h.
- Reserve (0.6h) + headroom to 6h absorbs an edit overrun or one slipped cut.
- Commitment: **1 essay + 3 cuts + 1 independent post per week.** No threads in
  month 1 — threads are the month-2 experiment slot.
- Per-surface cadence: newsletter 1/week, short-post platform 3/week, feed 1/week.

## 3. Pillars and ratios (16 slots across 4 weeks)

| Pillar | Target | Slots | Format notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frontend craft | 50% | 8 | essays + thesis/story cuts |
| Design engineering in practice | 31% | 5 | proof: teardowns, numbers |
| Studio notes (process/opinion) | 19% | 3 | perspective: essays, hot takes |

Named series: **"Interfaces that Age"** — 4 newsletter essays, one per week, each
dissecting how one durable UI pattern survives platform churn. Week 4 slot doubles
as the series finale + recap.

## 5. Repurposing map (anchor → cuts)

| Anchor | Cuts produced | Cuts skipped | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| W1-4 Persistent Toolbar | thesis (W1-1), number (W1-2) | story, framework graphic | essay had a strong claim + one measurement; no anecdote |
| W2-4 Survived Modal | story (W2-1), teardown (W2-2) | thesis | the story IS the thesis |
| W3-4 Navigation | contrarian (W3-1), thesis (W3-2) | list thread | thread reserved as month-2 experiment |
| W4-4 Finale | number (W4-1), recap teaser (W4-2) | contrarian | finale is already the contrarian piece |

Spacing: cuts publish 1-2 days after their anchor, never before; max one cut per
surface per day; angles rotated (thesis → story → number → contrarian) so the same
essay never hits a surface twice in one week with the same angle.

## 6. Batching workflow (the week, hour by hour)

- **Monday 2h:** outline all four week-N slots; write W(N+1) essay skeleton if one
  exists. Output: outlines, statuses → `outlined`.
- **Wednesday 2h:** draft the essay; second edit pass if time remains; otherwise
  essay finishes Thursday morning. Statuses → `drafted`.
- **Thursday:** newsletter publishes (scheduled Wednesday evening in the newsletter
  tool). Slot → `published`.
- **Friday 1h:** write the three cuts (they inherit the essay's material — this is
  why cuts are cheap), load all into native schedulers, verify queue, mark slots
  → `scheduled`.
- **Reserve 1h:** absorbs edit overruns or a slipped cut. Unused reserve rolls into
  next Monday's outline hour.

Buffer rule: next week's essay is outlined before this week's essay publishes. A
one-batch buffer means a bad Wednesday produces a Monday outline, not a missed
Thursday.

## 7. Weekly review — Week 2 (the one shown in full)

- **Shipped/slipped:** 4/0 — all slots published on their days.
- **What moved:** essay opens at the recent average; the story cut (W2-1)
  outperformed the account's recent average in replies; feed teardown (W2-2) was
  average for the surface.
- **Surprise:** the modal story post — replies asked follow-up questions the essay
  answers; the cut is pulling readers toward the anchor, which is its job.
- **Cost check:** 5.5h used; reserve untouched. Wednesday ran 20 minutes long.
- **The one change:** cut slots move from Friday to Thursday evening — cuts are
  inheriting fresh-essay energy and Friday hour was being spent re-reading rather
  than writing. One variable; next week's review judges exactly that.

## 8. Month-end review (condensed)

- **Keep:** series format (four essays held opens), Monday/Wednesday/Friday batch.
- **Iterate:** independent studio posts — 2 of 4 underperformed; next month they
  must come from client work with a specific decision in them, not general opinions.
- **Kill:** none this month. The feed cut on finale week (W4-2) is on watch: it
  pulled the weakest engagement of the month and gets one more appearance before a
  kill decision with a recorded reason.

## 9. What the month proves

The calendar held because capacity was measured before commitment (4.8h of 6h),
the series carried recognition while cuts carried reach, one slip would have been
absorbed by the buffer, and the review loop changed one variable at a time. The
failure mode this replaces — a 14-slot week planned on ambition — lasted nine days
in the counter-case and taught the audience that schedules were negotiable.
