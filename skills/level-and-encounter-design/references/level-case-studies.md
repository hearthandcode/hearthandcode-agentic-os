# Level Case Studies

*Case study.*

## Overview

Three published game levels, dissect beat-by-beat to reveal how they teach, pace, gate, and pay off. Each annotated at two grains: the level arc (macro) and the opening beats (micro). These are not intended to be copied — they demonstrate why the quoted principle works.

---

## Super Mario Bros. 1-1 (1985) — The Tutorial that Never Speaks

### Why it matters

1-1 is the gold standard for tutorial-by-level design. The entire genre derived from this level. It teaches run, jump, sprint, duck, stomp, block-breaking, power-ups, pits, and flagpole — all in 300 screen blocks.

### Arc Outline (estimate ~100 seconds speed run)

| # | Description | Teach: |
|---|---|---|
1 | Player starts on an empty platform, a Goomba moves. The arrow is left. | The player moving right. |
2 | A small gap with a ? block above; the block gives a coin if hit. | The player testing things: they aim upward to hit block. |
3 | A Goomba on the path. The player can jump or stomp. | First enemy encounter. |
4 | A pipe in the ground — skippable. The player is likely to avoid or jump on it. | First strange interaction: the pipe is decoration unless stepped on. |
5 | Four Goombas on the path; the field's first density. | Scaled-repetition: same enemy, higher count. |
6 | A high ? box, far jumps, pits, and death if misjudged. | The coin/box teaches hitting for blocks at height timing. Staring a pit jump without the ability to return means: first fall in real. |
7 | An elevated pipe — the player hits it upside — on the pipe is the warp zone. Secret. | The time-limited exploration offers a reward that ends the level. |
8 | The flagpole slide. | End state, screen transition. |

### Beat-by-beat analysis (first 30 seconds)

0:3sec — Player in black screen. Then player appears left.
>  **Tool:** the player is on the left, drawn to a clear open platform > Recognize: "I walk right".

3 seconds — reach? No. First enemy? Actually just space.
> And then the "walk right" continues to right.

4 seconds — a Goomba walks up. The first time the player sees an enemy.
>  The game gives the player many seconds to see it. It moves right, stops, then repeated. It's not a jump scare — it is a teach, without panic.

5-6 seconds — player uses A to jump. They may lived, or may kill or pass. 
> The stomp kills the enemy — the beep-a sound with the. Moment delights.

7 seconds — the ? block appears above the gap. The player has time to read and see.
> The block is placed exactly at the gap the player's first full jump. If the player jumps while moving, they hit the block.

First gap: pits come right after. The space shows empty space → the player will jump (especially after the block). A pit that is exactly jumpable.

**Key lessons:**
- The enemy that moves and waits: teaches the player that they can pause.
- The ? block: the teacher that the path is above the player as well.
- The gap + block: teaches that coin blocks can be hit while mid-air.
- The first death possible: at the pipe area (must jump small landing. The pipe platform is the 1st non-forgiving.)

### Why this still works

Direction: only one surface. No background mechanics. The colour tells only path/good. Every new thing is learn before the player is applied.

## Half-Life 2 — Ravenholm (2004)

Ravenholm is the horror-action level of Half-Life 2. It changes genre halfway — switches from open world driving to tight, inventory management on high-tension corridors.

### Level arc

| Estimated section | Threat level | Rest |
|---|---|---|
| Entrance by trolley | 2/10 — a remote vista, atmosphere | |
| The well (first headcrab) | No enemies: setting the space | 1/10 (functional) |
| The dark corridor + flickering lights | zombie spawns | none |
| Zombines (improved armored zombies) | crafting time | small |
| The gas-station floor puzzle | fuel trap sequence | none |
| The Church of Father Grigori | ** safe + ammo + weapon** | rest, 2 min |
| The tunnel + forest | 8/10 run for life | might not |
| Church escape battle | 10/10 (finale) | out.

### Micro-beat: The well descent

**Player step:** The player descends a ladder in to a well. Headcrab eat the corner. You shot off? **Player learns:** no feel safe? The headcrab can appear from any corner.

### Micro-beat: The broken elevator

The elevator ride reveals the setting: haunting silence, a view of destroyed town, sounds overhead. When elevators open: a zombie runs at the player. The chase is a release of four seconds of underpower.

### Key takeaways

2. **Context change:** Ravenholm changes the two-time from open-world to tight. Every level a player can guide a micro-generic shift.
3. **Environment tells a story:** Diorama on a table (a family's last dinner at a ravenblood) tells a story of its own.
4. **Safe hub:** Father Grigori, the weird savior, is a safe area PLUS refill. Player feel ready for the next part.

## Dark Souls — Undead Burg (2011)

### Why it matters

Undead Burg is the teaching prom for the entire Dark Souls ethos: deliberate, high-consequence, learn- and repeat-driven, connected world.

### Level arc (entry to Taurus Demon)

| # | Beat | Player lesson: |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Bonfire, small room, three hollows | Player learns to lock-on strafe. The hollow slowly attacks. |
| 2 | Second and hidden bonfire | teaches that death sends back, not to start. |
| 3 | Two pikemen on the bridge | player learns spacing proper; or die. |
| 4 | The bridge with dragon | Introduces spectacle and selective panic. |
| 5 | Undead burg proper — soldiers, crossbow, dog | Player must manage multiple threats. |
| 6 | Shield and chain invasion area | Ambush design: spawning behind you. |
| 7 | Taurus Demon boss | reinforcement them to climb tower to do the plunging attack. |

### Micro-beat: first door

The player starts at a door. Step through. Three slowly advancing enemies that move in place. The room is flat, medium length. The player can: stay, block, dodge. 

This teaches: 
- Melee targeting is prioritized to the nearest enemy.
- The fight feels threatening but is safe if you don't take risk.
- Estus flask allows one death recuperation.

### Micro-beat: The spear hollow

On the bridge. The spear hollow blocks half the narrow corridor. The player soon realizes they cannot rush in — but a backstep-dodge-target creates an opening. This is the game's first cue that: **a delayed time of an attack is a viabl window.** This is Dark Souls core.

### Architecture: Loops

The level wraps back on itself — a large hollow hub that cuts through a tower, connects to a bridge, goes under, the original ladder from the tower is unblocked. The level returns to a bonfire -> the player says "I know where I am. This is okay." The short cut is open.

**Lesson:** By closing the topology, the designer makes the player feel that the map is safe and known — even though it's still dangerous.

### Why

 Undead Burg works: It teaches combat by environment, not by text, and shows the player they can die anywhere, but there is a rest and a shortcut ahead. The player's growth within the level is the level's entire point.
