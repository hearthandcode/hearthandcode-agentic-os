# Facilitation Guide — Methodology

## The Facilitation Mindset

Your only job during divergence is to protect the idea flow. You are a gatekeeper for the rule. Your primary modes of operation are: note every idea (do not edit), ask clarifying questions only (never evaluative), and manage time.

You are not the idea generator. You are the idea host.

## Session Design Blueprint

### Preparation (15 minutes before the session)

**1. Define the prompt.**
A good prompt is specific, open yet bounded. "How might we create a podcast that listeners actively recommend to people who are not currently interested with the content?"
- "How might we encourage the newsletters that keep people opening and opening?"

**The prompt tests:**
- Would a 12-year old understand the prompt can generate 30 ideas?
- Is the prompt too restrictive? (but then generates too identical)
- Is the prompt under-constrained? (then the first idea might be far from the domain)

**2. Choose cues.**
- Select 2-4 divergence techniques from references/divergence-techniques.md.
- Plan the schedule with timing and technique for each 15-minute block.

**3. Prepare materials.**
- Sticky notes or digital equivalent with 10-second capture.
- A visible timer that everyone sees.

**4. Define the success criteria.**
- Something like: "By about 45 minutes we will have a pool of no less than 30 visible ideas."
- This should be spelled out — it is not "we will have a great idea."

### Running the Session

**Timeline for a 45-minute session:**

| Time | Activity | Role |
|------|----------|------|
| 0:00-0:05 | Warm-up: List 10 everyday problems in the related domain. No evaluation. | Get ideas flowing |
| 0:05-0:10 | Read the prompt aloud. Clarify any confusion. Re-state the success goal: at least 30 ideas. | Focus |
| 0:10-0:25 | Divergence technique #1. Timebox 15 minutes. Timer running. | Keep recording, keep the group on technique |
| 0:25-0:40 | Divergence technique #2. 15 minutes. | As above. |
| 0:40-0:45 | Quick cluster of the pool. | 5 minutes; do not define boundary of evaluation |

Total divergence: 30 minutes (2 techniques), more than enough for 30+ ideas.

### Energy Management

Signs the group is stuck:
- Long pauses: restate the prompt or switch technique
- Solutions that circle the same idea: the group is self-repeating
- Empty contributions: "pass" or "I don't know"

**Recovery moves:**
- "Take 2 minutes and write down 3 things that have nothing to do with this problem."
- Use worst-idea-first technique to break the seriousness.
- Force a constraint: "Now, do so with no budget," "Only this year," etc.

Signs of over-enthusiasm (group is producing but not deferring judgment):
- Group commenting on each other's ideas as they come in — remind them: each idea must stand at the divergence phase.
- Ambitious not: "That can't work..." "Instead..."
- Solution: "Stop signals" — just clear and anonymous and recorded.

### Energy Management: Solo

Solo sessions have the same basic flow. The solo practitioner must explicitly separate her own voice signals before each technique. The risk in a solo session is that one voice dominates — of course, there is only one, but different modes in one brain. This is done by physically leaving the room after the divergence session, then coming back to score it unemotionally.

### Remote Sessions

- Use a shared document or digital board visible to all participants.
- Use a voice timer: the facilitator sets a timer that everyone hears.
- Build in extra buffer time: 2 minutes for each transition.
- For async: use a forum format with a fixed 24-hour window. Brainwriting (Silent) is the only divergence technique that works fully asynchronously.
- For synchronous remote: maximum of 6 participants. More than 6 reduces the number of ideas per person sharply.

## Facilitation Traps

| Trap | Signal | Fix |
|------|--------|-----|
| Letting ideas get discussed | A participant says "good idea" or "bad idea" | "Hold that for convergence. Write it down. Next." |
| Allowing the domain expert to include more ideas | The domain expert talks above the norm | "Thank you for that, let's hear from someone else (lens)." |
| Letting one technique dominate | 60+ of the ideas have one structure | Force the next technique to be something completely different |
| Being the note-taker (not the facilitator) | Writing full sentences | Capture the idea in a phrase. Move on. |
| Implementing the idea rather than generating | "So we do it by the end of the month" | "Let's evaluate that in convergence. We are still generating." |
