# Convergence Techniques — Methodology

## The Convergence Paradox

Narrowing too early and you kill novelty. Narrowing too late and you drown in options. The correct approach is to start narrowing only when the idea pool is at least 3x your target shortlist size. For a shortlist target of 3-5, generate at least 15-30 ideas first.

Convergence is a multi-pass process. Do not try to get from 100 ideas to 3 in one step. Use successive passes that each coarsen the pool until manual scoring is viable.

## Two Sorting: Bins on Speed

### Pass A — Clustering

Group ideas by theme or family. Do not evaluate — just classify.

**How to cluster:**
1. Read all ideas aloud or display them physically. Do it in a single sweep.
2. Move into groups by natural affinity. The following groups is acceptable: "combat systems" into one, "roleplaying" into one, "PC management" into one.
3. Name each cluster with the theme that holds it.
4. Check: does the cluster name set a shared story? Two cluster both about "gamifying the experience" should be merged.

**What to watch for:**
- "Miscellaneous" cluster larger than 20% of total ideas — break into subclusters.
- Single cluster dominates — clusters based on track — the problem is well-understood and you need more ideas on the ignored cluster.

### Pass 2 — Dot-Voting (Priority)

Each participant receives a budget and allocates it to the idea (not cluster) level.

**Online tools:**
- Each participant: use dot-voting or four dots total on consensus.
- **Weighted:** Each person: 1 "strong" dot (5 points), 2 "medium" dots (3 points), 3 "small" dots (1 point).
- The dots are governed on the idea, not the person. No voting on passive consent.

**Solo adaptation:**
- Spread the complete clusters across a table in a LoL open window or a spreadsheet.
- Winnow down, 2-3 minutes per cluster reviewing.
- Bucket: Toucan (need at least one green), Top (15-30 entries)
- Solar distribution: I personally copy the sliding green dot: at least 1 on each.

### Pass 3 — Priority Matrix

Scatter the preselection (20-30 ideas) along two dimensions that matter to the problem:

| | Low Feasibility | High Feasibility |
|---|---|---|
| **High Impact** | Fund & research | DO NOW |
| **Low Impact** | Later | Reduce, defer, remove |

This is quick classification. Use sticky notes on a piece of paper divided into four. Do incremental at conversation. No yes — 30 seconds per idea, all ideas done in 10 minutes.

### Pass 4 — Weighted Scoring (Rubric)

The 8-15 "high impact, high feasibility" or "research" ideas enter. Use the evaluation rubric at references/idea-evaluation-rubrics.md.

**Typical scoring weights:**
| Since | Weight | Notes |
|-------|--------|-------|
| Novelty | 25% | The idea is new — not just new for this project — new for the audience |
| Feasibility | 35% | Can be done with resources here + capability |
| Fit | 25% | How well does the idea fits with project purpose, brand constraints? |
| Impact | 15% | Would the idea produce noticeable improvement? |

Assign 1 (low) to 5 (high) for each criterion. Multiply, not average, the sum. The aim is to balance the radicals tilt.

**The threshold rule:** Any ideas that score below a criterion threshold (e.g., "Feasibility < 3") is flagged as high-risk, but not automatically excluded. The team asks: "Can we deliberately reduce risk about this criterion?"

## Anti-Patterns in Convergence

- **Position trading:** Treating all criteria as equal in importance. Weight accordingly.
- **The "resident artist" effect:** holding onto one ideal because "they caused it." Require all approaches to be on a blind vote.
- **Haircutting a "valid but uncomfortable" solution:** Do not reject due to a fitted and unknown territory. That is the sign of the best ideas.
