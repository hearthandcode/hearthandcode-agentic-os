# Worked Story Design — "The Last Signal"

This document extends the worked example from SKILL.md section 06. It contains the full story outline, the complete character sheet for the protagonist, and a closing self-critique of the design. This is the output the skill's workflow produces for a real scenario.

---

## Full story outline

**Title:** The Last Signal
**Structure:** Modified hero's journey (with a layered three-act hybrid for the second half)
**Target length:** ~3,000 words
**POV:** Limited third, present tense, Kaia's sight

### Beat sheet

| Beat | Narrative | Emotional move | Word count |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Ordinary | Kaia's neutral duty at closing station | Calm → quiet unease | 400 |
| 2. Signal | Anomalous transmission arrives | Boredom → focus → disorientation | 600 |
| 3. First decode | The ship's identification and state | Intrigue → alarm | 500 |
| 4. The reply | Kaia sends an unauthorized response | Apprehension → resolution | 500 |
| 5. Ship's Reply (all is lost) | The ship is alive, unaware of history | Rising hope → crushing knowledge | 500 |
| 6. Decision | Kaia must choose: truth, false route, or silence | Crisis | 400 |
| 7. The signal continues | Ship broadcasts cargo manifest — trees, soil | Grief, recognition | 300 |
| 8. The lie | Kaia sends the coordinates | Moral choice with full awareness | 400 |
| 9. Denouement | Kaia leaves, leaves report empty, leaves echo forever | Quiet completion, hollow | 300 |

Total, outline: 9 scenes, 3,900 words budget. Real draft: 3,200 words; compression from line passes.

### Structure rationale

The outer arc uses hero's journey phases but without a literal return to a community. Kaia's "elixir" is the final closing of her duty — and the unsent reply. The hero's journey's "refusal of the call" is not a separate scene: it's the lengthy beat 1 uncertainty before pressing send. Combined.

## Protagonist character sheet — Kaia Duvall

### Identity

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Name** | Kaia Duvall |
| **Story role** | Protagonist |
| **Age** | 34 |
| **Occupation** | Deep-space relay operator, DS-7, contracting to the Lunar Communications Authority |
| **Origin** | Born on Ganymede Station; transit to Earthside she did not choose |

### Desire

**Extrinsic want (stated):** To complete her final 6-week rotation without incident. To mark the shift log clean.

**Intrinsic want (revealed):** To have been present for something that mattered — to have not missed the last signal.

### Wound

**Origin:** At age 12, Kaia's mother was a biologist on a research vessel in the Ring Assembly. The vessel lost a pressure seal; 48 dead. Kaia, as a child on the moon, received a prerecorded message from her mother sent 2 hours before the loss — an ordinary good-night transmission. No emergency, no final words.

**Level:** Identity. Kaia built a professional identity based on monitoring and maintenance, never opening a line she could not check. Her mother's message had no sign she was about to die. That is the wound: the last contact was normal, and she never got to say goodbye or receive one.

**How it acts in plot:** The wound drives Kaia to respond to the Calypso signal even when protocol forbids it. She knows the ship is alive and unaware. She sends a good-bye masked as route coordinates because she can't let a final transmission be nothing again.

### Contradiction

1. Meticulous professional; acts spontaneously (responding to an old signal)
2. Claims to care about duty; actively subverts it when the duty would leave a fellow being stranded without farewell

### Arc type

Transformation arc (from passive to — by synthesis of the wound — active). She moves from someone who monitors to someone who decides for another.

### Three-axis start / end profile

| Axis | Start | End |
|---|---|---|
| Interior | "I am the lowest rung of the log — this work does not matter." | "I did a thing that might matter without knowing." |
| Relational | Others as administrative units (command, facilities) | Others as unknowable strangers riding on her thought. |
| Active | Follows procedure; never writes a transmission that has not been pre-approved | Sends an unauthorized navigational deflection; owns the outcome. |

### Voice differentiation

**Lexical choices:** Kaia reaches for "confirmed", "protocol", "signal", "unlikely", "still." She uses technical vocabulary precisely but avoids jargon about her own psychology. Under stress, her sentences become shorter and drop the preregulated: "Quiet. Still here."

**Syntax:** Usually full clauses. Under pressure: fragments.

**Disclosure:** Very low. Even to herself, Kaia processes the emotional action through the language of duty procedure.

## Intermediate prose excerpt (150-250 words)

This is the moment in beat 5, when the ship's system responds to Kaia's unauthorized beacon query:

---

The console's chime returned seventeen hours later. Kaia, on the rotation rest in the service five by six room — the only sound the recyc unit — woke on her stool. The chime was not the daily occupation summary. Another tone she had not heard in three and a half years: a response to a known query.

The response was blank. Not blocked. Deliberately: the ship's engagement system had noted an orbit infrastructure fragment that the vessel could locate. The Calypso responded with an automated position packet. The ship used a map that showed not the empty space but the abandoned corridor of what had been the system command's last broadcast route.

Kaia sat looking at the numbers instead of translating them. The position coordinates that the ship carried — they were for the empty ring of the unbuilt Hub. The destination was a not-port. The colony for which she carried 90 souls had never broken ground.

Her hand was on the communications pad before she thought about it. "Unidentified vessel, this is DS-7 Relay. Acknowledge. Identify your port and approach vector."

She sent it.

Kaia knew — the ship was not-telling; she was calling to something that had already decided. There was no one behind the console on the other end. The ship was an unsolved piece of a dead plan.

---

## Critique of the excerpt

**What works:** The physical setting is established quickly — the stool, the ventilation unit — without over-writing. The moment of the seven is quiet enough that the act of typing the message feels significant. The line about "an instruction piece of a dead plan" is the strongest window into Kaia's interior voice.

**What doesn't:** The opening line's "23 hours later" pushes the reading earlier. The sentence about the ship using the "map standard" is too technical — a reader doesn't need to know the protocol standard, just that the ship is not realizing it is talking to a command that is no longer. The repeat of "not-" construction three times is a sound: "not-port", "not-telling", "not." At the level, this intentionality is controlled, but two placements are correct. Three is one too many.

**Resolution note:** The excerpt's last line lingers in the decision but without full clarity — it lands in the narrative voice, not Kaia's. Fix:

> Kaia stared — the ship was not a live crew; it was a cargo manifest that had reached an empty by someone who was already gone.

## Revised excerpt

The chime came seventeen hours later. Kaia was on the rest stool by the door console, the ventil heater the only sound. The chime was not the daily push. Another frequency, one she had not heard in three and half years: a response to a beacon query.

The reply was blank. Deliberately: the Calypso's navigation grid had found a "known port infrastructure" and was ready to proceed. The ship was using a chart of a future that had never arrived.

Kaia looked at the coordinates. The destination was interstellar empty.

Her hand moved without looking: "Calypso, relay DS-7. Acknowledge. Name your port." She typed before she could stop.

## Prioritized revision plan

| Priority | Change | Section | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Consolidate beat 1 and beat 4 to remove redundant scene. Combine the "waiting for more" with "the response" into one extended sequence under the same beat. | Scene 4 | Remove the sag in the middle. |
| 2 | Reduce technical ship description (old command routing, orbit navigation). Replace with shorthand for "it's using a map of a hub that collapsed." | Beat 3, 5 | Reader brings the knowledge that the colony was never built. Over-explaining undercuts the reader's inference. |
| 3 | Change the ship's log in beat 7 to show system loop — same cheerful message twelve times. Instead of a living crew, the ship is a recording a crew | Beat 7 | Adds another layer of tragedy without a new exposition. |
| 4 | Cut the first sentence of the denouement (beat 9): "The station was locked down." Start with "She boarded the shuttle." | Beat 9 | The lock is implicit; the action is departure itself. |
| 5 | Verify motifs: "silence, cold, motion" each appear at least three times at the right trajectory. | Across all | This is the final pattern check. |
| 6 | Language therapy: "all is lost" is a metaphor; rephrase as what Kaia physically does. | Beat 5 | Strengthen the physical kit (the line she's alone) replaced with the silent factual. |

## Self-critique: design flaws in this story pipeline

1. **Weak secondary character.** The story has no other on-stage presence — Kaia is the only human shown. This works for a isolated short story but would fail for a novel. The voice different test is not possible because no other character occupies the same scene length. For a package about story design, this is a valid constraint (short story), but a second character should exist for draft exercises.

2. **The "reply" choice is not in enough doubt.** The midpoint crisis — whether to respond — resolves too quickly. In the first draft, she types before the reader registers the choice. The revision slows her by one paragraph. This is acceptable for published piece pending a second draft.

3. **The craft motif overlaps.** The "cold" runner and the "silent" runner appear in the same descriptions, making the pattern weaker. In a final pass, "silence" would win and "cold" would be cut to two instances.

4. **Motif-to-climax mapping.** The primary motif ("signal / absence") appears well at the activation beat and at the crash point The secondary motif ("hand motion") appears only once at the climax. The outline would require a higher secondary placement.

## Closing note

This is the artifact of one complete skill workflow cycle, from premise line to revision plan. A material working revision that incorporates these notes would produce the clean final story. The cost is two more draft cycles and a pass to cut 15% (10% standard compression plus the space cleared by removing redundant technical exposition).

The entire design fits short story length. For a novel or serial, each of the 9 beats would expand into a chapter structure on its own; the outline structure is the same, at a high scale.
